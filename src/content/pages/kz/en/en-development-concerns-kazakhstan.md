## In brief

If you are concerned about your child's development, you do not need to choose a diagnosis or treatment method on your own. Begin with a specific question: what has changed, which skill is difficult, and how does it affect everyday life?

In Kazakhstan, medical developmental assessment and PMPK assessment have **different purposes**.

## First, identify what you need

| Your question | Where to start |
| --- | --- |
| Speech, communication, movement or general development | The primary healthcare organisation where your child is registered |
| A further medical assessment | Primary healthcare, with referral when clinically indicated |
| Possible autism and a medical diagnosis | Healthcare, including specialist services where appropriate |
| Adjustments for learning and psychological and educational support | PMPK |
| An acute condition posing a threat to life or health | Emergency medical services: **103** or **112** |

## Step 1. Record specific observations

Before your appointment, note examples over several days:

- how your child expresses needs or discomfort;
- how they understand spoken language;
- how they play and interact with other people;
- how they move and use their hands;
- how they eat and sleep;
- whether they have stopped using a skill they previously used.

It helps to record **when** changes began and **in which situations** they occur. Discuss loss of acquired skills with a healthcare professional without unnecessary delay. Losing a skill alone does not allow anyone to judge the urgency of the situation remotely.

Saying "I think my child's development is different" is important, but concrete examples help the professional understand what you are seeing.

## Step 2. Discuss development at the polyclinic

Kazakhstan's current paediatric care standard provides for child development and early intervention centres within primary healthcare, child development rooms and psychophysical screening.

This makes the child's polyclinic a reasonable first contact for questions about health, development and medical referrals.

A practical question to ask is:

**"Where does our healthcare organisation refer a child for developmental assessment and early intervention?"**

The staff available and the process of accessing them can differ across regions.

**Important:** screening helps identify possible risks and the need for further assessment. It does **not**, by itself, establish or rule out a medical diagnosis. Additional investigations should have a clear clinical purpose.

## Step 3. Consider educational assessment separately

If your question concerns special educational conditions, psychological and educational support or a suitable learning pathway, an assessment by **PMPK** may be relevant.

PMPK is Kazakhstan's psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation service. PMPK and medical assessment **are not interchangeable**. A PMPK referral or conclusion should not be understood as proof of a particular medical diagnosis.

Use the current PMPK state service rules, including the 2026 amendments. Older leaflets may contain outdated lists of required documents or service timeframes.

## If a diagnosis has not yet been established

Support for specific difficulties does not always need to wait until the entire diagnostic process is complete. For example, a child may need help with communication, eating, the sensory environment or an everyday learning goal.

Medical causes of symptoms should still be assessed through healthcare. Discussing support before diagnosis **does not automatically entitle** a family to every state service. Some services require a doctor's referral, a PMPK conclusion or another legal basis.

## When urgent help is needed

If a child deteriorates suddenly, loses consciousness, has difficulty breathing, experiences a seizure or has another immediate threat to life or health, do not wait for a scheduled PMPK appointment or session at a developmental centre.

In Kazakhstan, call **103** for an ambulance or **112** for emergency services.

## What to bring to the first appointment

Bring a short timeline of your observations, any available medical reports, previous test results and a list of medicines your child takes, if applicable.

There is no need to pay for a broad set of tests before a clinician has identified a specific reason for them.

## What to avoid

Do not:

- assume that one screening score proves or rules out autism;
- substitute PMPK for medical assessment;
- wait for a final diagnosis before discussing functional difficulties and appropriate support;
- pay for multiple investigations without a clear clinical purpose;
- use outdated requirements for a government service without checking its current rules.

## Your next step

If your concern is about health or development, contact your child's registered primary healthcare provider and bring your observations.

If educational adjustments are the main question, ask about PMPK assessment. These pathways may proceed in parallel.

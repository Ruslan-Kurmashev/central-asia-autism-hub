## In brief

There is **no single type of kindergarten** that every autistic child must attend. The practical question is what conditions the child needs to communicate, participate, learn and remain safe, and which setting can realistically provide them.

## What preschool options may be available?

Depending on the child's educational needs and what is available in the region, families may consider:

- a general preschool or kindergarten;
- a specialised group;
- a specialised preschool institution.

The best route should **not be chosen on the basis of diagnosis alone**.

## Where should a parent start?

Contact a kindergarten you are considering, or the relevant local education authority. Ask about the current admissions process, available places and forms of support.

Administrative procedures for general and specialised preschool education may differ. A diagnosis alone does not guarantee a place at a specific institution.

If a child needs special educational conditions, discuss assessment of those needs and **PMPK recommendations**. Medical diagnosis, educational assessment and the kindergarten's admission decision are **different processes**.

## What does PMPK do?

PMPK is Kazakhstan's psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation service. It assesses special educational needs and gives recommendations about educational programmes and psychological and educational support.

This does not replace a medical assessment. PMPK findings are primarily used to inform an educational pathway and the support required.

## What is psychological and educational support?

Rules governing the psychological and educational support service were approved under **Order No. 92** and amended by **Order No. 144-NҚ of 29 May 2026**.

These rules expressly cover preschool education. The work may concern early social participation, play, communication and developmental needs.

They provide for identifying difficulties, assessing special educational needs, advising educators and families, and establishing appropriate psychological and educational conditions.

The level and form of support should depend on the child's individual needs, not on an identical package of sessions for all autistic children. This also does **not** mean that every kindergarten will have the same professionals or resources.

In practice, ask:

- Who belongs to the support team?
- Which needs have been identified for the child?
- What adjustments will actually be made?
- How will the child's communication be supported?
- What will staff do during sensory overload or a difficult situation?
- How will the family receive feedback?

## Conditions worth discussing

A more useful question than "Are you inclusive?" is **"What will you do in these specific situations?"**

For example:

- Can the child use **augmentative and alternative communication (AAC)**, picture cards or a visual schedule?
- Is there a quiet space where the child can recover from sensory overload?
- How do adults support transitions between activities and help the child understand instructions?
- How are mealtimes and toileting needs managed?
- Who is responsible for support within the group?
- How are goals and progress documented?

## Questions to ask when visiting the kindergarten

1. Who is responsible for providing support and communicating with the family?
2. How will the need for adjustments to the learning programme and environment be assessed?
3. Which communication methods are accepted and actively supported by staff?
4. What will staff do if the child experiences sensory overload, anxiety or difficulty participating in the group?
5. Will there be a written individual plan with goals and dates for review?
6. How are eating, hygiene, rest, safety and handover to parents organised?
7. How will parents learn about progress, difficulties and any changes to the support plan?

## A specialised kindergarten is not automatically the best option

A specialist institution may have greater access to relevant resources, but whether it suits **this particular child** still needs individual consideration.

Likewise, a general preschool group does not automatically provide appropriate support simply because it calls itself "inclusive".

## Warning signs

Seek clarification if someone tells you that:

- admission requires purchasing private therapy;
- AAC or other communication methods are prohibited without a clear justification;
- the autism diagnosis automatically means the child must be isolated from peers;
- parents cannot be told the goals or arrangements of the support;
- the same intervention is compulsory for every autistic child.

## Your next step

Before choosing a kindergarten, write down **three to five specific conditions** that your child needs to participate in a normal day. Compare potential settings against those needs rather than the name of the programme alone.

## 1. Purpose of the Framework

The document helps professionals across services consistently assess a child's actual functional strengths, environmental barriers and support needs. Its use does not depend on the child having a diagnostic category.

Unlike the [Australian autism diagnostic guideline](../autism-crc-australia-assessment-diagnosis/), the Framework **does not answer whether a child meets autism diagnostic criteria**. Its aim is to identify needs and improve communication of their description between services.

## 3. Why ICF and F-Words matter

The Framework draws on the International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF). The official FAQ also uses the CanChild F-Words concept: functioning, family, fitness, fun, friends and future.

Functioning is considered in interaction with living conditions. A high level of support need in one situation does not necessarily imply a particular medical diagnosis.

| Concept | What to discuss with the child and family |
| --- | --- |
| Functioning | Which everyday activities are accessible and where help is needed |
| Family | How family life is organised and what support is useful |
| Fitness | Physical health, activity and access to movement |
| Fun | Interests and preferred activities |
| Friends | Relationships and opportunities for communication as desired by the child |
| Future | Aspirations and future needs |

These are prompts for discussion, not a test, diagnostic criteria or autism severity scores.

## 4. Assessment and prioritisation are not the same

**Assessment** describes strengths, functioning and existing support. **Differentiation** means identifying the most important needs and acceptable options for action together with the child and family. The term does not mean clinical differential diagnosis of ASD here.

During assessment, first gather available assessment results and observations from families and professionals, then identify missing information. There is no need to repeat an instrument merely to complete a new report if existing data are current and sufficient. Reassessment may be necessary when health, environment or needs change.

### Selecting tools

There is no universal set of tools for every child. Selection takes account of purpose, age, language, cultural context, accessibility and psychometric properties. Standardised methods can be supplemented by observations, interviews and information from different settings. Professionals must be able to interpret results and identify limitations, including a lack of established local validity.

## 5. Written reports: professional significance

For communication between services, a report can usefully reflect:

- the views of the child and family, including accessible communication methods
- strengths and meaningful interests
- functional needs in a particular environment
- agreed priorities and limitations
- the need for review when circumstances change.

This is an AutismHub editorial list, not the official Autism CRC form. The [Strengths and Support Needs Tool](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/best-practice/strengths-and-supports/tool) is a fillable PDF that can be used digitally or printed. The developer provides a Quick Reference Guide and requires registration to obtain the files. The form is intended to communicate agreed information about needs, not to calculate diagnostic scores.

### Functional assessment example

AutismHub scenario: a child participates in activities at home but often moves to a quiet place in a noisy group. Assessment includes the child's views, observations across settings, existing communication methods and environmental information. The family and child may select comfortable participation in a chosen group activity with an accessible way to request a break. The report describes needs, agreed environmental adjustments and a review date, rather than a suspected diagnosis or fixed therapy intensity.

## 6. Differences from other Australian documents

| Document | Main purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Autism CRC diagnostic guideline, second edition | Organising clinical autism diagnosis |
| Autism CRC supporting children guideline (2023) | Selecting, delivering and evaluating support |
| **Framework (2024)** | Consistent assessment of functioning and needs in children with any developmental characteristics |

## 7. Application in Kazakhstan

Principles of respect for the child, shared assessment and clear reporting can be useful to professionals. However, the Framework does not change national rules on medical diagnosis, PMPK activities, medical and social assessment, consent or data sharing.

For legal and organisational procedures, use the [catalogue of Kazakhstan documents](../kazakhstan-documents/), checking the current versions.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Framework version 1.1, December 2024, has been checked.
- [ ] Functional assessment is not replaced by ASD diagnosis.
- [ ] Child and family preferences and environmental conditions are considered.
- [ ] Goals and needs are documented in understandable language.
- [ ] Instruments used match professional competencies and linguistic context.
- [ ] The professional decision complies with Kazakhstan's national requirements.

**Limitation:** this Framework review is not a clinical prescription and does not determine entitlement to services, benefits or disability status.

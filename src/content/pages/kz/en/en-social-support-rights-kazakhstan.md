## In brief

After an autism diagnosis, families often ask: "What benefits does a child with autism qualify for?"

A more reliable question is: **What legal status has been established, which social protection measures have been identified and which government services apply to this particular family's circumstances?**

A diagnosis and entitlement to a specific benefit or service are **not the same thing**.

## Where to start: three different questions

| Your question | Main pathway |
| --- | --- |
| Do we need a medical assessment and possible referral for disability evaluation? | Treating clinician and the medical organisation's medical advisory commission (**VKK**) |
| Do we need determination of disability status and individual support measures? | **Medical and social expert assessment (MSE)** under the applicable rules |
| Do we need benefits or specialised social services? | The relevant government service, depending on the legal status, documents and eligibility conditions |

**An autism spectrum disorder (ASD) diagnosis does not, by itself, establish disability status** or guarantee any particular benefit.

Healthcare and education processes can take place in parallel, but **PMPK**, Kazakhstan's psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation service, does not replace MSE.

## MSE and disability determination

**Medical and social expert assessment (MSE)** follows specific rules and is used to determine disability-related questions and necessary social protection measures.

Under points 4-9 of the MSE Rules, a referral from the medical organisation's medical advisory commission (VKK), with the required consent to data processing, provides the route to MSE. The referral uses form No. 031/u and is based on functional impairments and limitations in daily activities, not an autism diagnosis alone. The general rule for children's first referral specifies a period no earlier than four months after diagnosis, subject to exceptions. Confirm the applicable timing with the treating clinician and VKK.

For a child's MSE assessment, the PMPK conclusion is submitted **if available**, not as a blanket mandatory document. The rules allow in-person and certain remote assessment procedures.

Families should not be promised a particular disability classification, duration of status or set of support measures solely on the basis of an autism diagnosis.

The MSE rules were updated by **Order No. 329 of 11 August 2026**, with changes effective from **25 August 2026**. The precise information and documents required depend on the circumstances. The PMPK conclusion is considered **when available**, not as a universal mandatory document for every application.

## What is IPAR?

**IPAR** is the individual rehabilitation and habilitation plan for a person with a disability (Kazakhstani Russian abbreviation: `ИПАР`).

It identifies particular measures for rehabilitation, habilitation and support.

For disability-related rehabilitation, assistive devices and support, check whether a measure is included in IPAR and which further requirements apply. IPAR is not a universal prerequisite for every specialised social service based on other legally recognised grounds of need. Article 157 of the Social Code uses the general term "individual programme".

## Which documents and details should be checked after MSE?

Confirm whether:

- a formal MSE decision has been issued
- disability status has been established
- any relevant duration or review date has been specified
- the necessary measures are included in the IPAR.

If the decision contains an error or unclear wording, seek clarification through the authorised agency and the prescribed procedure. **If you disagree with the MSE expert conclusion**, point 54 of the MSE Rules allows an administrative appeal no later than three months after receipt. The rules also distinguish appeals against service-delivery actions from challenges to an expert conclusion. Court review normally follows the required pre-trial administrative stage.

Also check which measures may be arranged **proactively** and which require an individual application. Avoid sending scans of personal documents to private intermediaries without checking whether they are needed and how the information will be handled.

## State benefits

Kazakhstan's **Social Code** provides for state social benefits and a separate allowance for a person bringing up a child with a disability.

In particular, distinguish:

- the **state social disability benefit**, and
- the **allowance for a person raising a child with a disability**.

These are **different grounds for entitlement and different award procedures**. Article 170 of the Social Code lists general forms of social protection. The **allowance for a person raising a child with a disability** is regulated specifically by **Article 183 (entitlement)** and **Article 184 (amount)**, the state social disability benefit is addressed in **Articles 175-177**.

**Applying for benefits:** consult the separate government pages for the [state disability benefit](https://www.gov.kz/services/3000?lang=ru) and the [allowance for a parent or guardian raising a child with a disability](https://www.gov.kz/services/3030?lang=ru). Order No. 215 states that the latter is awarded independently of household income. It has a distinct application or proactive service route. Confirm any proactive offer and bank details through official channels.

The standard decision period on a complete benefit application is **seven working days**, though specified exceptions or requests for additional documents may alter this period. This is not the time needed for the MSE assessment. Amounts must be checked against current official figures rather than presented as permanent rates.

## Specialised social services

**Specialised social services** may be provided in different settings, including residential, day-care or other semi-residential arrangements, and support delivered at home. Articles 131-133 and 140 of the Social Code set out different grounds for recognising an individual or family as needing services, followed by a needs assessment by a social worker. These include early psychophysical developmental limitations among children under three and disability or persistent functional impairments. No child automatically qualifies for home-based services merely through an autism diagnosis.

The Code distinguishes a guaranteed package of services funded by the state for those found eligible from additional services that may be paid for by the recipient. Each setting and category also has its own service standards.

This is a distinct part of the social protection system. Not every private autism intervention programme is a "specialised social service" within the legal meaning of that term.

Eligibility, assessed needs, the service provider and regional availability matter when considering a particular form of support.

Specialised social services are not restricted solely to people who have an established disability status. Kazakhstan's Social Code recognises different circumstances in which an individual or family may be assessed as needing specialised social services. Eligibility and the extent of support depend on an individual needs assessment under the applicable rules. If a child has not been determined to have a disability, the family can still ask the local social protection authority whether another recognised basis for assessment applies. This does not guarantee entitlement to a particular service or a free place.

## Where to find reliable information

Use the current versions of Kazakhstan's [Social Code](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/K2300000224) and [MSE rules](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2300032922). For an application, follow the relevant government service page on **eGov.kz** or **gov.kz**.

Verify the requirements immediately before applying. Some legal amendments have **delayed commencement dates, including provisions scheduled to take effect in 2027**, they must not be treated as already in force.

For specialised social services, identify the specific format needed, such as support at home, semi-residential support or residential services, and check eligibility and local availability. **Home-based services have particular requirements:** the [official guidance](https://www.gov.kz/situations/234/intro?lang=ru) may require further supporting documents, including PMPK conclusions for certain children. General Social Code grounds do not override these service-specific rules.

## A checklist for families

1. Which **official status** has already been established?
2. Which documents were issued after MSE?
3. Is there an IPAR, and which measures are included?
4. Which **specific government service** is needed: disability benefit, parent/guardian allowance, or specialised social services needs assessment?
5. Where can it be requested?
6. Which information can the government system retrieve automatically, and which documents must be supplied?
7. Are there regional conditions, limited places or a waiting list?

## Why there is no single universal list of "autism benefits"

Eligibility may depend on the child's age, disability status, IPAR, family circumstances, the particular service and the region.

A list without these qualifications can quickly become outdated or create misleading expectations.

## Your next step

If the family is only beginning to consider disability assessment, start by discussing the **VKK and possible MSE pathway** with the relevant healthcare provider.

If disability status has already been established, review the **IPAR** and identify the specific government services that apply.

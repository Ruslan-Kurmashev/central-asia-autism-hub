PECS (Picture Exchange Communication System) is a structured form of augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) that teaches a person to initiate communication independently by handing another person a picture representing an object or activity.

**PECS does not mean every kind of picture card, and it is not the whole of AAC.** Visual schedules, choice cards, gestures, communication boards, writing and speech-generating devices may all support communication in different ways. PECS is not a prerequisite for using a tablet or any other AAC system.

## How PECS works

The developers describe a protocol with six successive phases. Teaching starts with independently exchanging one picture for a desired item or action. Later phases address approaching a communication partner in different settings, distinguishing pictures, constructing a simple sentence, answering questions and commenting.

This is a structured teaching protocol, not merely a collection of printed cards. The phases are described on the [official PECS website](https://pecs.com/picture-exchange-communication-system-pecs/).

### An illustrated example of exchanging a picture

Imagine a child who wants to play with a ball. The child selects a picture of a ball, hands it to an adult and receives a response to the message. The important feature is **independently initiating an exchange with another person**, rather than simply pointing to a picture.

## PECS and other picture cards are not the same thing

A **visual schedule** shows the order of events, a **choice board** helps someone select an option, and a **"break" card** can communicate an important need. These supports can be useful without following the full PECS protocol.

### What pictures can support communication?

Pictures can show particular objects, activities or needs. The symbol should make sense to the individual and be easy to access. Some children understand drawings best, while others prefer photographs of familiar things.

![Six illustrated communication cards with English labels: WATER, APPLE, BALL, BOOK, WALK and MORE.](/central-asia-autism-hub/images/editorial/pecs/pecs-example-request-cards-english-owner-approved-2026-11-08.webp)

**Communication must not be limited to requesting objects.** People need ways to say no or stop, report pain, ask for help, request a break and say that they need the toilet.

### What is a sentence strip?

Later PECS phases combine pictures. For example, the images for "I want" and "ball" can form a meaningful request.

## Who might benefit from PECS?

PECS has most often been studied among autistic children and children with other developmental disabilities, particularly those with limited functional speech. It may be considered when communicating through pictures suits the individual, they need a way to initiate messages independently and their current communication methods are insufficient.

A diagnosis, age or lack of speech does not, by itself, establish that PECS is the best option. Compare AAC approaches and consider vision, movement, symbol understanding, family languages, preferences and use across everyday settings.

## What does research show?

PECS studies have produced varied findings. More independent requests should not be confused with improvements in speech, broader social communication or quality of life.

### Earlier studies and the Cochrane review

A 2010 meta-analysis included eight single-case experiments and three group studies. It found small-to-moderate communication gains but no convincing overall improvement in speech. Maintenance and generalisation were also insufficiently studied.

In a British school-based randomised trial involving 84 children, PECS training for teachers was associated with more communication initiations and picture use. There was no significant increase in speech frequency or standardised language scores, and some gains were not maintained after active support ended. A follow-up analysis found increases mainly in spontaneous requests for items and activities.

A Cochrane review identified one eligible randomised PECS study and assessed the certainty of the evidence as **very low**. Its search ended in 2017, so it does not cover more recent research.

### Newer evidence from 2026

A 2026 meta-analysis included 37 randomised trials from mainland China. Of these, 34 entered the quantitative synthesis, with 2,343 participants overall. The authors reported a positive pooled effect for communication skills: **SMD 0.94, 95% confidence interval 0.67-1.21**.

For language development, the pooled effect was not statistically significant: **SMD 0.44, 95% confidence interval -0.53 to 1.41**. SMD means standardised mean difference, not the percentage of children helped.

Results from one geographical and service context cannot automatically be transferred to families in Kazakhstan. Training conditions, professional preparation, participant characteristics and outcome measures may differ. Independent studies and verification of specific conclusions are still needed.

**Bottom line:** PECS may improve some communication outcomes, particularly initiation and requests. It does not guarantee the same benefit for every child or all forms of communication.

## PECS and spoken language development

PECS is sometimes marketed as a method to "get a child talking", but this is too strong a promise. The 2010 meta-analysis, the British school study and the pooled findings from 2026 did not demonstrate a convincing general effect on spoken language or broader language development.

This does not mean an individual child's speech can never change. The **primary goal of PECS is accessible functional communication, not guaranteed spoken language**.

## PECS must not restrict other AAC approaches

According to [ASHA](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/augmentative-and-alternative-communication/), PECS is one possible AAC approach. **There is no requirement to complete PECS before accessing gestures, a communication board, a tablet or a speech-generating device.**

If a child communicates through speech, gesture or another device, that message must count. Do not remove another communication system to force a picture exchange or make picture exchange a condition of water, pain relief, toilet access, help or a necessary break.

Physical prompting requires particular caution. If the child shows fear, resistance or substantial discomfort, stop coercive prompting and consider a less intrusive support approach.

## Must someone complete all six phases?

Reaching phase six does not automatically establish successful communication. A 2024 systematic review found variation in mastery criteria across studies, and many participants did not complete the full sequence.

It matters more whether the system supports independent communication with different people and in different places. If another AAC method is easier to use, there is no need to continue PECS merely to complete the protocol.

## Quality of implementation and limitations

A 2024 review covering 49 studies on PECS implementation found that training, setting and supervision were often insufficiently described. Therefore, promising to "follow the protocol strictly" does not guarantee an outcome.

Practical limitations arise when communication remains restricted to asking for objects, the person depends on constant prompts, pictures are used only with one professional, or a more accessible AAC method is not offered. Consider access to refusal, reporting pain, questions, comments and personal interests too.

A system should not be continued merely because considerable time has already been spent learning it.

## How to tell whether PECS helps

Before starting, select **1-3 meaningful goals**, such as independently asking for help, refusing, requesting a break, initiating contact or commenting on an event.

Record the starting point, then monitor independent messages, the number of prompts, the range of communication purposes and picture use with different partners at home, school and elsewhere. Also note usability and significant distress.

**Do not assess success only by the number of exchanges, attempts or the phase completed.** Ask whether it has become easier for the person to communicate independently what they actually want to say.

## When should PECS be reviewed?

Revisit the plan if communication remains limited to requests, prompts are not being reduced, the system is used only in the therapy room, picture exchange is uncomfortable or other AAC options become more accessible.

Changing a communication tool is not a failure. Maintaining access to communication matters more than maintaining a particular protocol.

## Questions to ask the professional

1. **Choice.** Why was PECS suggested, and what other AAC approaches were considered?
2. **Goal.** Which specific communication difficulty is the programme intended to address?
3. **Independence.** How will prompting decrease and self-initiated messages be supported?
4. **Range of messages.** How will the person refuse, report pain, ask questions or comment?
5. **Everyday use.** How will use with different people and outside sessions be assessed?
6. **Outcome.** When and on which measures will the plan be reviewed?
7. **Qualifications.** What foundational professional training does the provider have and what additional PECS training have they completed?

## Considerations in Kazakhstan

PECS may be used in educational, psychological, medical and private services, but the name of a service does not establish a provider's qualifications. Check the person's education, actual professional role, competencies and the requirements applying to a particular regulated service.

**A PECS course certificate does not replace foundational professional qualifications or demonstrate that the service will help a particular child.**

## In brief

PECS is one specific AAC approach, not a synonym for any picture card. Research supports some functional communication outcomes, particularly initiating contact and making requests, but does not guarantee spoken language development.

Evaluation should focus on accessible and independent communication, a range of message functions and generalisation to everyday life. PECS is not a prerequisite for other AAC systems.

## 1. What NICE CG128 addresses

CG128 helps organise the steps from initial reasonable suspicion to explaining assessment findings and identifying subsequent support. It is **not a guideline for selecting therapy**. NICE provides separate guidance on support in CG170.

Practitioners should distinguish three stages: recognising characteristics, assessing the need for referral and conducting a comprehensive diagnostic assessment. They cannot be combined into one step simply because a questionnaire or diagnostic tool has been used.

## 2. Recognising characteristics and discussing concerns with families

In recommendations [1.2.1-1.2.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations), NICE advises considering possible autism when developmental or behavioural characteristics are present, taking parents' and children's observations seriously and recognising that individual characteristics may have other explanations.

In practice, this means:

- record **specific situations and observations**, rather than concluding that a child is definitely autistic based on one behaviour
- clarify whether characteristics occur across settings and identify the child's strengths
- discuss concerns respectfully, without pressure or blame
- consider the language of communication and possible sensory difficulties during interactions.

Loss of previously acquired skills requires particular attention. Under [1.3.1-1.3.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations), children younger than three with regression in language or social skills are referred to an autism team. For language regression from age three or loss of motor skills at any age, assessment by a paediatrician or paediatric neurologist comes first, with onward referral if needed. This describes the NICE pathway, not referral rules in Kazakhstan. Regression requires timely medical consideration.

NICE also warns that autism may go unrecognised in girls and that, in older children, characteristics may be compensated for by acquired strategies or a supportive environment. Point 1.2.3 helps prevent assessment being refused solely because the presentation is atypical.

## 3. Screening is not diagnosis

Screening questionnaires and other tools may help structure observations, but **a positive result does not confirm a diagnosis and a negative result does not rule it out**. Recommendation [1.3.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations) emphasises this distinction.

For example, a high screening score may prompt discussion of further assessment. It does not justify independently making a definitive diagnosis, overlooking co-occurring conditions or promising that a child will not need assessment following a negative result.

The tool must also be appropriate to the person's language, age and abilities. Translating a questionnaire without adequate adaptation and training does not guarantee that its psychometric properties will be retained.

## 4. Who participates in the diagnostic pathway

CG128 provides for a team approach and access to different professional competencies. In the UK model, the core team includes a relevant medical specialist, a speech and communication specialist and a psychologist with appropriate experience ([1.1.3-1.1.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations)).

Tasks may include:

1. Reviewing the referral and determining the need for diagnostic or alternative assessment.
2. Gathering information on development, health, communication, adaptation and living circumstances.
3. Observing and using appropriate tools with trained practitioners.
4. Discussing findings, uncertainty and differential assessment together.
5. Explaining conclusions and necessary next steps to the family.

**The composition of the UK team should not be mechanically copied into the staffing structure of an organisation in Kazakhstan.** Current arrangements for specialist services in Kazakhstan need to be checked.

## 5. What comprehensive diagnostic assessment includes

Recommendation [1.5.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations) describes a multidimensional assessment. Its purpose is not to identify one correct test, but to compare different sources of information.

| Component | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| **Developmental history** | When characteristics emerged and how communication, play, skills and interaction developed |
| **Views of the family and child** | Specific concerns, needs and everyday situations |
| **Observation** | Communication and behavioural characteristics in context |
| **Medical information** | Physical health, developmental history and possible co-occurring conditions |
| **Differential assessment** | Other conditions or combinations of factors that could explain some observations |
| **Profile of needs and strengths** | Support needed regardless of the wording of the diagnosis |

An observation protocol does not remove the need to assess differential and co-occurring conditions. The report should reflect not only difficulties but also accessible communication, strengths and context.

### Medical investigations

Under section 1.7 of CG128, medical investigations are not routinely ordered solely to establish autism. Genetic investigations are considered on individual indications, such as congenital anomalies, dysmorphic features or intellectual disability, with specialist advice. EEG is considered when epilepsy is suspected. The absence of routine testing does not mean withholding investigations when clinically indicated.

## 6. After assessment: conclusions and communicating findings

NICE recommends preparing a written report with conclusions and their rationale, discussing findings with the family and, when necessary and agreed, sharing important information with other involved services ([1.8.4-1.8.7](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations)).

The UK pathway includes organisational targets: starting diagnostic assessment within three months of referral ([1.5.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations)) and offering a follow-up appointment within six weeks of completing assessment for children and young people diagnosed with autism ([1.8.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations)). **These are NICE provisions for its own system, not legally confirmed deadlines in Kazakhstan.**

If diagnosis remains uncertain, reconsideration in light of new information and discussion of a second opinion are provided for where needed. Uncertainty does not remove the person's need for accessible support.

## 7. Practical example

**Situation:** a school psychologist is approached by the parents of a child experiencing communication difficulties and marked anxiety in class.

Appropriate professional practice does not involve independently diagnosing autism from a questionnaire. First document observations, the family's views, the school environment, communication needs and available developmental history. Then discuss referral to a competent diagnostic service under the local pathway. While diagnosis is being clarified, the school can consider reasonable environmental adjustments within its authority.

**What CG128 provides:** the principle of comprehensive multidisciplinary assessment. **What it does not provide:** a ready-made legal referral pathway for Kazakhstan.

## 8. Brief checklist before using CG128

- [ ] The document date and version are established.
- [ ] The age and question fall within CG128's scope.
- [ ] Screening is not presented as an established diagnosis.
- [ ] Possible alternative and co-occurring conditions are considered.
- [ ] Assessment is conducted by a competent practitioner or team.
- [ ] The family and child are involved in discussions where possible.
- [ ] Current local procedures and professional authority are checked separately.
- [ ] Clear next steps and a way to explain findings are established.

## Limitations and applicability in Kazakhstan

CG128 is **an international reference**, not a standalone regulatory instrument registered in Kazakhstan. Before applying specific provisions, check national clinical protocols, diagnostic rules, licensing and competencies, availability of practitioners and local service arrangements. Requirements for educational institutions and medical organisations may differ substantially.

The guideline must not be used to promise a specific diagnosis date in Kazakhstan, claim validity for unadapted tests or bypass the need for qualified in-person assessment.

## Main conclusion

NICE CG128 is particularly useful **as a model for systematic, comprehensive and carefully documented diagnostic assessment**. It helps practitioners distinguish observation from diagnosis, coordinate team activity and provide understandable feedback to families. Implementation must account for national rules and individual circumstances.

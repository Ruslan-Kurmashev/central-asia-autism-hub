## In brief

If a centre claims to provide healthcare, check its licence **through the official government service**, not through a photograph on its website or social media profile.

Kazakhstan's eGov/eLicense system allows you to search for permits and licences **free of charge**.

## What information to prepare

Ideally, have one or more of the following:

- the organisation's **business identification number (BIN)**;
- the **individual identification number (IIN)** of a sole proprietor, where applicable;
- the licence holder's official legal name;
- the licence or permit number.

A BIN is often more useful than the advertised brand name because the brand and legal entity may have different names.

## How to check the licence

1. Open the [official eGov page for checking permits and licences](https://egov.kz/cms/ru/services/proverka-razresheniy-i-licenziy).
2. Select the online-service option to proceed to Kazakhstan's **eLicense** electronic licensing portal.
3. On eLicense, select the function for **searching permit documents** (`Поиск разрешительных документов`).
4. Try the quick search using the BIN, IIN, document number or other available identifiers.
5. If there are too many results or none appear, use the advanced search. Enter the registered name, licence holder, type of activity and any other available criteria.
6. Open the document record. Compare the holder, document number, status and permitted activity with the contract and the service being advertised.

Official eGov guidance updated **7 April 2026** states that searching issued permits and licences on eLicense **does not require an account or registration**. Different rules may apply to other transactions on government portals.

### How the search works

The eGov service page leads to eLicense's document search. Quick and advanced search options may include IIN, BIN, document number, licence holder, issuing authority, type of activity, issue date and current status.

The official interface may change, so follow the current labels shown on the portal.

## What to examine in a result

Check at least four details:

1. **Who owns the licence?** Does the legal entity or sole proprietor match the party receiving payment?
2. **What is the current status?** Is the document currently valid?
3. **What is covered?** Does it authorise the actual medical activity being offered?
4. **Are there annexes or additional authorisations?** Do these match the claimed healthcare services?

Finding a document with a similar title is not enough.

## When to stop before paying and ask for clarification

Pause the process if:

- the licence belongs to a different legal entity than the one named in the contract;
- the status indicates that its operation is suspended, terminated or invalid;
- the licence exists but does not cover the medical activity being offered;
- the provider shows only an image and will not provide the BIN, licence number or identity of the licence holder.

**A missing search result alone is not proof of illegal activity.** Double-check the legal name, BIN, search criteria and the precise service before drawing conclusions.

## Licence statuses

The government service can display statuses corresponding to *valid*, *invalid*, *terminated*, *suspended* and other conditions.

A screenshot from an earlier date is therefore not a substitute for checking the current record.

## Important: not every service requires a medical licence

Speech and language sessions, teaching support, psychological services and healthcare fall under **different regulatory and professional frameworks**.

It would be incorrect to conclude that every activity of a centre is illegal simply because it does not hold a medical licence. First establish what activities it actually provides and how the law classifies them.

## A licence is not proof of effectiveness

Licensing concerns whether an organisation may lawfully undertake a particular regulated activity.

It does **not** establish whether a proposed intervention is effective for autism or suitable for a particular child's goals. That requires separate consideration of scientific evidence, risks and expected outcomes.

## If the information does not match

Do not rush to accuse a provider publicly. First request the registered name, BIN, document number and clarification of which legal entity is entering into the agreement.

If doubts remain, use an official government contact or complaint channel to clarify the information.

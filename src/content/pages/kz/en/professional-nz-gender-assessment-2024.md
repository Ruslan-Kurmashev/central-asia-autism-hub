## 1. Why the supplement was needed

Clinical recognition of autism may depend on the populations in which early characteristics were studied and on how practitioners interpret communication. Relying on a single stereotypical image of an autistic person increases the risk of missing other presentations.

Whaikaha examined research on sex, gender, the timing of diagnosis and ways of masking autistic characteristics.

## 2. Main findings of the supplement

The official announcement highlights several principles for practice:

- recognise the diversity of autistic characteristics across individuals
- avoid relying on a single stereotypical profile when considering diagnosis
- compare an individual's characteristics with those of non-autistic peers of the same gender and comparable intellectual abilities
- recognise that masking may occur across ages and identities
- set support goals that allow people to participate safely and authentically in life.

These are **professional recommendations in the supplement**, not new independent diagnostic criteria.

The official summary specifically notes girls and women, transgender and non-binary people, and people with a fluid gender identity: their presentations may differ from the stereotypical male profile. This highlights the risk of diagnostic stereotypes rather than establishing new test cut-offs. [Official summary](https://www.whaikaha.govt.nz/news/news/new-supplementary-paper-updates-aotearoa-new-zealand-autism-guideline).

## 3. Masking and limits of interpretation

Masking refers to attempts to conceal, modify or compensate for observable behavioural and communication characteristics in response to circumstances or social expectations.

For some people, apparently successful adaptation comes with substantial internal effort. A practitioner must not conclude that a person is autistic solely because they mask or belong to a particular group.

## 4. Errors to avoid in assessment

| Assessment error | Better-supported approach |
| --- | --- |
| Expecting only stereotypically male presentations | Consider different individual profiles |
| Treating successful outward adaptation as proof that support is unnecessary | Gather developmental history and consider context and internal effort |
| Equating masking with diagnosis | Treat it only as a possible context for clinical assessment |
| Drawing conclusions from one interview or questionnaire | Use a comprehensive professional assessment |

### Practical notes from AutismHub

When communication appears to be going well, it is worth exploring developmental history, the effort required to communicate, differences between behaviour in safe and demanding environments, the use of rehearsed phrases and the need to recover after social interaction. With the person's consent, information from different settings can be compared. These are examples of questions for comprehensive assessment, not a separate validated Whaikaha instrument. A single response about masking cannot establish a diagnosis.

## 5. Involving the person

During diagnostic interviews and support planning, consider the person's preferred language, conditions for safe communication and their right to express their own needs.

The aim is not to make someone appear more autistic to obtain a diagnosis, but to provide a fair assessment based on clinical criteria and developmental history.

## 6. Application in Kazakhstan

The document can help professionals recognise diverse autism presentations and reduce stereotypes. It does not determine who is authorised to diagnose, which tools are compulsory or which procedures apply in Kazakhstan.

For national requirements, see the [catalogue of Kazakhstan documents](../kazakhstan-documents/). For the full clinical context, consult the [main New Zealand guideline](../new-zealand-national-autism-guideline-2022/).

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Clinical assessment does not rely on a single stereotypical image of autism.
- [ ] Age, gender, cultural context and developmental history are considered.
- [ ] Masking is not treated as proof of diagnosis.
- [ ] The person's dignity and preferences are respected.
- [ ] Findings are assessed against clinical criteria and professional competence.
- [ ] The rules of Kazakhstan are checked independently of the foreign document.

## 1. What changes after diagnosis?

A diagnostic conclusion alone does not answer questions about the child's support, health and quality of life. CPS emphasises the role of the paediatrician or another responsible clinician in coordinating subsequent surveillance, referring to other specialists and discussing emerging difficulties.

Support should not be reduced to a single course of sessions. Actual needs change with age, educational circumstances, family circumstances and co-occurring health conditions.

## 2. Main areas of follow-up support

| Area | What the document considers |
| --- | --- |
| Health surveillance | Identification and management of co-occurring conditions |
| Communication | Need for speech and language support and AAC |
| Everyday functioning | Independence, participation and environmental adjustments |
| Mental health | Anxiety, attention, mood and behavioural difficulties |
| Family | Education, information and caregiver support |
| Social and educational settings | Access to services and continuity during transitions |
| Outcome monitoring | Observing meaningful changes and reviewing support |

CPS considers medical, psychological, educational and social aspects of support. Specific job roles and organisational responsibilities depend on the local system.

## 3. Co-occurring health needs must not be overlooked

The original discusses dental problems, gastrointestinal symptoms, restricted eating, anxiety, ADHD and other conditions separately. When a child finds it difficult to communicate pain or discomfort, their responses may mistakenly be interpreted as purely behavioural.

Practitioners should assess concerns promptly, consider common medical causes of symptoms and refer to a specialist physician when indicated.

**An ASD diagnosis alone does not explain pain, sleep disturbances, worsening nutrition or other new symptoms.**

Routine preventive surveillance includes hearing and vision assessment according to need, dental care and discussion of nutrition, sleep and mental health. A restricted diet may warrant assessment for deficiencies and specialist consultation. Medical procedures should account for communication accessibility, sensory sensitivity and opportunities to prepare the child for the visit. A new symptom requires separate assessment rather than attribution solely to autism.

## 4. Supporting communication and daily living skills

The document discusses speech and language therapists, psychologists, occupational therapists, physiotherapists and other professionals when their work matches the child's needs.

Accessible communication is an important part of professional support, including AAC for children who need alternative or additional ways to express needs and participate in life.

AAC use should not automatically be equated with abandoning spoken language development. Communication tools and learning formats should be selected according to individual goals and available evidence.

## 5. Intervention selection and monitoring

CPS emphasises that **there is no universal programme for all children**. Even within one approach, outcomes depend on the child's characteristics, goals, practitioner qualifications and delivery conditions.

Professional analysis should include:

- relevance of the goals to the child and family
- evidence of expected benefits and limitations of that evidence
- accessibility and acceptability of the approach
- practitioner competencies and quality of supervision
- monitoring outcomes, demands and possible adverse effects.

References to particular approaches in the document reflect the scientific literature in 2019. They do not automatically demonstrate that one programme is superior in 2026.

### Evidence after publication of the CPS statement

The updated Project AIM systematic review (Sandbank et al., 2023) covers non-pharmacological interventions for young children and evaluates different approaches and outcomes separately. The authors found positive effects for some developmental and naturalistic developmental behavioural interventions, but findings depend on outcome type and risk of bias. Adverse events were inadequately monitored in many studies, limiting reliable comparison of the benefits and harms of each intervention. This is a separate source, not a revision of the CPS text. Its search covered publications through November 2021, so it also does not represent all literature through 2026. [Original Project AIM publication](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076733).

When selecting support, agree an observable functional outcome, acceptable demands and review timeframe in advance, and monitor adverse changes.

## 6. Behaviour that signals difficulties

When self-injurious actions, aggression, severe distress or other unsafe situations occur, initial assessment should consider possible pain, communication barriers, mental health, environmental overload and changes in familiar circumstances.

CPS discusses functional assessment of behaviour and environmental adjustments as part of a shared plan. Restrictive practices or medication should not automatically be the starting point without assessing causes and risks.

In severe distress, consider possible bullying, abuse, routine changes and age-related transitions. CPS also recommends discussing risks of a child leaving a safe place and vulnerability to abuse in advance. A safety plan should account for individual circumstances, accessible communication and protection of the child's rights.

Urgent individual clinical situations require direct assistance from qualified professionals.

## 7. Medicines and alternative approaches: scope of this review

The 2019 original addresses pharmacological management of some co-occurring conditions and discusses alternative approaches. **This is not a recommendation to use medicines or supplements without a physician.**

AutismHub does not reproduce doses or regimens. Any clinical decision requires newer specialist guidance, safety assessment and verification of availability and registration of the relevant products in Kazakhstan.

Publication or mention of an approach in the CPS text does not itself establish its effectiveness or safety.

In its section on complementary and alternative approaches, CPS 2019 specifically warns about risks and lack of established benefit from chelation, hyperbaric oxygen and secretin for autism. Antibiotics and antifungals should not be used to treat core autistic characteristics. This does not apply to treatment of infections when medically indicated. Facilitated communication, where an assistant may determine message content, must not be confused with independent AAC use. Restrictive diets and supplements do not replace necessary support or treatment. When a condition or deficiency is identified, follow the relevant specialist recommendations.

## 8. Family and life transitions

The original discusses family support when moving into school or another organisation, changes in home circumstances and needs for social resources.

Useful practice includes agreeing in advance who shares necessary information, how the child expresses preferences, who has access to assessment results and when the team reviews goals. Information sharing must comply with applicable consent and data protection rules.

Canadian support programmes, compensation and benefits vary by province and period. They cannot be described as current forms of state assistance in Kazakhstan.

CPS recommends regularly discussing physical and mental health of parents and other caregivers, respite needs, sibling support and access to social resources. When needs are identified, offer appropriate referral rather than merely advising the family to organise the child's sessions better. Specific Canadian payments and services must not be transferred to Kazakhstan without verification.

## 10. Professional checklist

- [ ] Specific child and family needs have been identified after diagnostic assessment.
- [ ] Co-occurring medical conditions, sleep, nutrition and mental health are considered.
- [ ] Goals, responsible practitioners and outcome assessment methods are clear.
- [ ] The child's communication preferences, including AAC needs, are considered.
- [ ] When behaviour causes difficulties, pain, context, environment and communication accessibility are assessed.
- [ ] Medication decisions are not based on an outdated foreign text without current medical assessment.
- [ ] The plan is reviewed during life transitions.
- [ ] National requirements and professional authority in Kazakhstan have been checked separately.

**Scope:** the document concerns early identification of various developmental disorders, including ASD, hearing difficulties, motor and intellectual impairments. It is not exclusively about autism and does not replace specialist diagnostic assessment.

## 1. Purpose of this clinical report

AAP considers child development a continuous process requiring attention at every preventive visit. Practitioners should discuss family observations, document development and use standardised instruments when needed.

The key difference from the [AAP report on ASD](../aap-2020-autism-identification-evaluation-management/) is its broad scope: it describes **how to recognise a wide range of developmental characteristics**, rather than how to diagnose and support autism specifically.

## 2. Three distinct levels of identification

| Stage | Purpose | What cannot be concluded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developmental surveillance | Repeatedly gather information on the child's development and circumstances | Does not itself constitute a diagnostic assessment |
| Standardised screening | Use a valid standardised tool to identify risk | A positive result does not mean a diagnosis has been established |
| Comprehensive evaluation | Clarify causes of difficulties and necessary support | Cannot be reduced to a screening questionnaire |

Surveillance includes the family's views, developmental history, professional observations, consideration of risk and protective factors, documentation of findings and sharing necessary information with other practitioners under applicable rules.

## 3. AAP screening ages: an important distinction

| Age | AAP general developmental screening | AAP separate ASD screening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9 months | Yes | Not specified as a standard universal screening age |
| 18 months | Yes | Yes |
| 24 months | Not one of the three universal general screening ages | Yes |
| 30 months | Yes | Not specified as a standard universal screening age |

**Important:** this schedule reflects AAP preventive paediatric practice in the US, not the current mandatory schedule of Kazakhstan. Where complaints or reasonable concerns exist, assessment should not be artificially delayed until the next scheduled age-based visit.

### Before school entry

AAP recommends paying particular attention to development at the preventive visit at age 4-5 years before school starts. Standardised screening is conducted when concerns, risk factors or identified difficulties are present. This is not an additional universal age for autism screening. When substantial risks or developmental disorders are already established, the routine screening algorithm should not replace necessary specialist assessment.

## 4. When concerns arise

The report describes a clinical pathway rather than merely making an entry in the medical record:

1. Clarify which characteristics the family and practitioners have noticed.
2. Conduct standardised screening if needed or refer for specialist assessment.
3. Consider early support and related services if significant difficulties have already been identified.
4. Agree on follow-up surveillance and documentation of progress.

General screening may identify needs unrelated to ASD, so its results should not be interpreted as an autism-specific indicator.

When a screening result is concerning, further medical and developmental assessment and consideration of referral to early support services are necessary. If concerns persist, even a reassuring screening result does not remove the need for further surveillance or referral when indicated. At the next contact, check whether the referral was completed and whether the recommended services are accessible to the family.

## 5. Role of the family, early childhood education and other practitioners

AAP particularly emphasises information sharing between paediatric services and early childhood practitioners. Decisions on data sharing, consent and confidentiality in Kazakhstan must follow applicable national legislation.

The practical value of this document is that development is assessed using information from different situations, not just a brief observation in a consulting room.

## 6. Critical application of recommendations in Kazakhstan

| Transferable professional principle | What cannot be transferred automatically |
| --- | --- |
| Regular surveillance and respect for family concerns | Ages 9/18/30 months as a mandatory local rule |
| Distinguishing general and ASD-specific screening | US payment and referral pathways |
| Responding to identified difficulties without unjustified delay | Legal status of US screening tools in Kazakhstan |
| Documentation and coordination | US professional authority and information-sharing rules |

Kazakhstan's regulatory framework is presented in the [health care documents catalogue](../kazakhstan-documents/healthcare-diagnosis/).

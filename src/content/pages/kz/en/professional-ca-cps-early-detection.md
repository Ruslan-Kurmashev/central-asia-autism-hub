## 1. Purpose of the document

CPS considers early detection part of continuous developmental surveillance. Family information, professional observations and characteristics of communication, behaviour or development may justify further assessment. No individual sign alone confirms or rules out ASD.

This is the first of three related CPS statements. The subsequent documents address **standards of diagnostic assessment** and **post-diagnostic management**. Detection, diagnosis and support remain distinct professional tasks.

## 2. Developmental surveillance and screening: what is the difference?

| Process | Purpose | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developmental surveillance | Clarify family concerns, developmental progress and clinical observations at every appropriate visit | Surveillance is not a diagnostic test |
| Standardised screening | Use an age-appropriate instrument to identify increased likelihood of difficulties | A positive result does not establish diagnosis |
| Targeted assessment of increased risk | Systematically clarify autistic characteristics and consider referral | Does not replace subsequent clinical diagnosis |
| Diagnostic assessment | Determine whether clinical findings meet diagnostic criteria | Requires appropriate professional qualifications |

CPS calls for careful attention to parental reports even when no standardised questionnaire result is available.

CPS identifies the preventive visit at 18 months as an opportunity for more detailed developmental assessment and discussion with parents. Developmental questions are raised at every visit, not only at this age.

## 3. Important difference between Canada and the US

The American AAP separately recommends universal autism-specific screening at 18 and 24 months. **The CPS 2019 document does not formulate an identical universal age schedule.** It emphasises regular developmental surveillance for all children and more focused assessment when risk or clinical concerns are present.

CPS also discusses uncertainty about the benefits of universal standardised screening in children without previously known risk factors. This discussion reflects evidence at the time of the 2019 publication.

Do not transfer the American ages of 18 and 24 months to CPS as a mandatory Canadian rule or claim that CPS opposes all screening.

A separate 2016 Canadian Task Force on Preventive Health Care document recommends against standardised developmental delay screening in children aged 1-4 without signs, increased risk or parental or clinical concerns. This concerns general development, not a prohibition on indicated autism-specific screening. [Original CTFPHC document](https://canadiantaskforce.ca/guidelines/published-guidelines/developmental-delay/).

## 4. Early signs and clinical suspicion

The document gives examples that may require assessment: difficulties in reciprocal social communication, limited sharing of gestures and interests, unusual sensory responses, repetitive actions or loss of previously acquired skills.

**The combination of signs and developmental history** matters, not a checklist of one or two items. In some children characteristics become clearer as social demands increase. Surveillance therefore continues after a negative early screen.

## 5. When to consider referral

CPS proposes more focused assessment at increased risk. Referral for diagnosis may be justified by:

- a valid screening result indicating further assessment is needed
- persistent concerns of parents or other caregivers
- clinical observations raising high suspicion
- a combination of risk factors and emerging characteristics.

The document mentions M-CHAT-R/F, ITC, STAT and other instruments. This **does not mean** each is automatically validated in Russian or Kazakh, appropriate at any age or usable without specific training.

### Instruments listed in the CPS 2019 table

| Instrument | Age according to CPS | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M-CHAT-R/F | 16-30 months | Parent questionnaire and follow-up interview |
| ITC | 8-24 months | Early communication questionnaire |
| STAT | 24-36 months | Observation during play-based tasks |
| RITA-T | 18-36 months | Brief interactive assessment |

This is not a mandatory test list. Interactive instruments require appropriate training. Age ranges come from the historical CPS table, and current developer instructions should be checked. Accuracy estimates from individual samples cannot be transferred to other populations without verification. M-CHAT-R/F includes a second stage where indicated, and the initial questionnaire is not a completed two-stage screen.

## 6. Practical pathway after signs are identified

1. Discuss observations and reasons for seeking assessment with the family without premature diagnostic statements.
2. Clarify age, developmental history, context and existing assessment findings.
3. Arrange professional diagnostic assessment where indicated.
4. In parallel, consider support for already identified needs within available services.
5. Maintain follow-up surveillance, particularly when uncertainty remains.

For the next clinical stage, CPS provides the separate document [Standards of diagnostic assessment](https://cps.ca/en/documents/position/asd-diagnostic-assessment).

### Referral and subsequent surveillance

A referral can usefully record the reason for seeking assessment, family and professional observations, pregnancy, birth and developmental history, risk factors, relevant medical information and previous assessment results. Persistent concerns after a negative screen require further surveillance, and high clinical suspicion warrants diagnostic assessment. CPS recommends considering early support for children at increased risk before diagnosis is completed. Specific services depend on needs and the available local pathway.

## 7. Limitations in Kazakhstan

| Useful principle for comparison | What cannot be transferred without verification |
| --- | --- |
| Continuous developmental surveillance | Canadian visit schedule as a Kazakhstan requirement |
| Attention to parental views | Canadian referral procedures and professional authority |
| Targeted clinical risk assessment | Foreign cut-offs without local language validation |
| Referral and follow-up | Canadian funding and access arrangements |

For legally significant procedures, use the [Kazakhstan health care documents](../kazakhstan-documents/healthcare-diagnosis/) and check current primary sources.

## In brief

A business calling itself an "autism centre" or showing numerous certificates on its website does not tell you **who** will actually provide the service or **what professional authority** they have to do so.

A reliable check starts with one question: **What type of service are we being offered?**

## Step 1. Identify the type of service

A single organisation may offer several services, such as:

- healthcare;
- psychological support;
- teaching or educational support;
- social services;
- learning sessions;
- consultations or commercial programmes.

It would be misleading to apply exactly the same legal test to every professional.

## Step 2. If the service is medical, check the licence

Healthcare activities are subject to specific licensing rules. Do not rely solely on a screenshot of a licence supplied by the centre.

The official [eGov service for checking permits and licences](https://egov.kz/cms/ru/services/proverka-razresheniy-i-licenziy) links to **eLicense**, Kazakhstan's electronic licensing portal. The state lookup is free and offers quick and advanced searches, including by business identification number (BIN), individual identification number (IIN), document number and status.

Check **more than whether a document exists**. Compare:

- the full legal name of the licence holder;
- the licence's current status;
- the authorised type or subtype of medical activity;
- any relevant annex or additional licence details.

A licence issued to a different legal entity, or for a different medical service, does not establish permission to deliver the advertised service.

For practical steps, see [How to check a medical licence through eGov and eLicense](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/en/help-kazakhstan/check-medical-license-kazakhstan/).

## Step 3. If it is psychological support, check current requirements

Kazakhstan adopted a separate **Law on Psychological Activity** in 2026, setting a legal framework for psychological services.

Law No. **332-VIII of 2 July 2026** enters into force 60 calendar days after its first official publication, with a separate commencement date of **1 January 2028** for Article 17. Article 21 provides a **one-year transitional period** for psychologists to register in the state registry from the date the law takes effect.

Therefore, **not being listed in a registry that is still being implemented does not, by itself, establish a legal violation**. Check the applicable date, transitional rules and current procedures. Specialist medical care remains subject to its own laws.

You can review the [Law on Psychological Activity on Adilet (official Russian text)](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/Z2600000332).

## Step 4. For teaching services, check the role and employer

Qualification requirements for a teacher, special educator, speech and language professional or another education role depend on **the position and the type of institution**.

Order No. **338**, covering standard qualification characteristics for educational posts, includes amendments made in **June 2026**. Whether those rules apply depends on the person's actual role and organisation, not only on a business name containing the word "centre".

A short commercial training certificate does not automatically replace relevant qualifications or education required for a regulated role.

## Check the organisation and practitioner separately

A centre may have a valid legal registration while the child works with a particular individual. Ask for both organisational details and the professional's:

- full name and actual role;
- field of qualification;
- evidence of relevant education;
- registration, licence or other legally required documents where applicable.

If someone promises to "diagnose autism" or another medical condition, ask **who** will make the diagnosis and **what legal basis** allows them to do so.

A non-medical psychological or educational report must not automatically be represented as a medical diagnosis.

## Questions to ask a centre

Before paying, request:

1. The organisation's full registered name and BIN.
2. The practitioner's name and professional role.
3. Details of their primary professional education.
4. The licence number and annex, if the service is medical.
5. Clearly defined goals of the proposed work.
6. How progress and outcomes will be assessed.
7. Information on risks, limits and alternatives.
8. The total cost and whether the programme can be discontinued.
9. The documents recording fees, payment and contract terms.

## Documents worth requesting before payment

Ask for a written description of the service, the duration and cost of a session and any package, cancellation and refund rules, how the child's personal information will be processed, and a payment receipt or equivalent evidence.

If a centre claims measurable outcomes, ask **which goal** will be assessed, **using what measure**, and **over what period**.

Checking documents can reduce risk but **does not guarantee quality or results**.

## Warning signs

Be cautious when a provider:

- claims it can "cure autism";
- guarantees results for every child;
- sells a large package before assessing the child's individual goals;
- refuses to explain staff qualifications;
- presents a course attendance certificate as a substitute for professional credentials;
- claims a licence proves an intervention is effective;
- will not explain fees, session length or termination rules.

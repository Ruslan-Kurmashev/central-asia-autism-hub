When autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is suspected, families often encounter a long list of proposed assessments: ADOS-2, EEG, MRI, blood tests, consultations with a neurologist or geneticist, and more.

It is important to distinguish two purposes.

**Diagnosing ASD itself** is based on a psychiatrist's clinical assessment, the child's developmental history and diagnostic criteria.

**Additional investigations** help clarify the developmental profile, assess co-occurring conditions or investigate another medical hypothesis.

To learn who can establish a medical diagnosis and how the diagnostic pathway works, see [How is autism diagnosed in a child in Kazakhstan?](../how-autism-is-diagnosed-in-kazakhstan/).

## The essentials in one minute

No single laboratory test, brain image or psychological instrument independently confirms or rules out autism.

| Method | Purpose | Required for every child? | Does it independently diagnose ASD? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psychiatrist's clinical assessment | Medical diagnosis | **Essential stage** | **Yes, the psychiatrist establishes the diagnosis** |
| Developmental history | Diagnostic information | **Essential part of assessment** | Part of diagnosis |
| ADOS-2 | Standardised observation | No | No |
| ADI-R | Structured interview about developmental history | No | No |
| Psychological assessment | Developmental profile and psychological functions | When needed | No |
| Speech and language assessment | Speech and communication | When needed | No |
| Hearing assessment | Possible hearing difficulties | Depending on the situation | No |
| Neurologist | Neurological symptoms and conditions | When indicated | No |
| EEG | Electrical activity of the brain | When indicated | No |
| MRI / other neuroimaging | Specific neurological questions | When indicated | No |
| Laboratory tests | Physical health and other medical questions | When indicated | No |
| Genetic investigations | Possible genetic cause or co-occurring condition | When indicated | No |

Therefore, **"first ADOS-2 + EEG + MRI + laboratory tests + genetics, then a psychiatrist"** is not a universal mandatory pathway for every child.

## Is ADOS-2 required?

ADOS-2 is a standardised observation procedure that provides structured information about social interaction, communication, play and other characteristics associated with ASD.

Kazakhstan's clinical ASD protocol No. 145 lists ADOS-2 and ADI-R as supplementary diagnostic methods. The need for such methods is determined during the diagnostic process.

This has two important implications.

**Not having ADOS-2 does not automatically make a medical ASD diagnosis impossible.**

Conversely:

**An ADOS-2 result is not itself a medical diagnosis.**

It must be considered alongside developmental history, clinical observations and other available information.

A useful question for the psychiatrist:

> "Do we need ADOS-2 in our particular situation, and what additional information would it provide?"

## What is ADI-R?

ADI-R is a structured interview with a parent or another person who knows the child's developmental history well.

It helps systematically gather information about early development, communication, social interaction and repetitive behaviour.

Kazakhstan's clinical protocol, like with ADOS-2, classifies ADI-R as an additional diagnostic method.

ADI-R can therefore supplement the clinical assessment, but it does not replace the psychiatrist's medical judgement.

Parents need not arrange both ADOS-2 and ADI-R independently merely because autism is suspected.

## Is psychological assessment needed?

A psychological assessment can help build a clearer picture of a child's development.

Depending on the clinical question, the professional may assess:

- overall developmental level
- cognitive functions
- attention and memory
- thinking and reasoning
- emotional and behavioural characteristics
- strengths and difficulties.

The ASD clinical protocol includes experimental psychological assessment as a supplementary method.

Psychological assessment can be especially useful when the clinician needs to understand whether observed difficulties mainly concern social communication or are part of a broader developmental delay.

ASD and intellectual disability can co-occur. Neither excludes the other.

A psychologist's report alone, however, is not a medical ASD diagnosis.

## Does intelligence need to be tested separately?

The ASD clinical protocol does not make a separate intelligence test a universal mandatory condition for diagnosing every child.

Assessment of intellectual or general cognitive development may be useful where there are signs of broader developmental delay or the clinician needs a more precise understanding of the child's developmental profile.

The purpose is not to obtain one number to "prove autism", but to understand development in a wider context.

## Is a speech and language specialist needed?

Speech and language assessment may help determine:

- how well the child understands spoken language
- how they use words and phrases
- how they use language to communicate
- what other speech or language differences are present.

This matters because a speech delay and autism are not the same condition.

A child can have considerable speech difficulties without ASD. A child with ASD may also have a separate speech or language disorder.

It is therefore incorrect to conclude **"the child does not speak, so they must be autistic"**. Diagnosis considers a much broader picture of development and behaviour.

## Should hearing be assessed?

When speech is delayed or there are questions about a child's responses to sounds, hearing assessment can be important.

A hearing difficulty may affect:

- response to being addressed
- understanding speech
- development of spoken language
- participation in communication.

A hearing assessment can help establish whether hearing difficulties explain some of the observed challenges.

However:

**a hearing test is not an autism test.**

Hearing differences and ASD may also co-occur.

## When is a neurologist needed?

A neurological consultation is particularly relevant when a child has signs that require a separate assessment of the nervous system.

Examples include:

- seizures
- unusual episodes of staring or reduced responsiveness
- substantial loss of previously acquired skills
- changes in motor skills
- other neurological symptoms.

A neurologist helps assess these conditions, but that role is distinct from a psychiatrist's authority to make a medical diagnosis of a mental or behavioural disorder.

## Is EEG mandatory?

There is no basis for treating electroencephalography (EEG) as a universal mandatory test for every child with suspected ASD.

EEG measures the electrical activity of the brain and may be especially relevant if a clinician suspects epileptic activity or another condition for which EEG is informative.

EEG may be considered, for instance, when:

- seizures have occurred
- unusual episodes of loss of responsiveness are observed
- significant regression of previously established skills has occurred
- other signs raise concern about possible epileptic activity.

EEG does not independently confirm or rule out autism.

A more useful question is:

> "Which symptoms or findings make EEG appropriate for my child?"

## Is brain MRI mandatory?

MRI can assess brain structure, but standard MRI is not a diagnostic test for ASD.

Neuroimaging may be needed if the clinical picture suggests a separate structural, degenerative, demyelinating or other neurological condition.

MRI should not be performed solely to "see autism on a scan".

A normal MRI does not exclude ASD, and an MRI finding does not independently establish an ASD diagnosis.

## Are blood and urine tests needed?

There is no routine blood or urine test that independently confirms or excludes autism.

Laboratory investigations may be prescribed for other purposes, such as:

- assessing general physical health
- investigating a specific disease
- monitoring the safety or tolerability of medication
- testing a specific medical hypothesis.

It helps to distinguish:

**tests performed in a child with ASD**

from

**a test for ASD**.

The former may be medically indicated. There is no universal test of the latter kind.

## Are genetic investigations needed?

For some children, genetic assessment may be clinically useful, particularly if the clinician suspects an inherited condition or wants to clarify a possible biological explanation for developmental characteristics.

Kazakhstan has a separate clinical protocol for whole-exome sequencing. The groups discussed in that protocol include certain children with suspected inherited diseases, psychomotor developmental delay, ASD and particular neurological conditions.

This does not mean whole-exome sequencing or any genetic panel is mandatory for every child with suspected ASD.

Genetic assessment may be especially relevant when there is also:

- substantial global developmental delay
- intellectual disability
- congenital features or malformations
- an unusual neurological profile
- marked regression
- suspicion of a genetic syndrome
- a significant family history of inherited disease.

The scope of testing must be guided by the specific medical question.

Also remember:

**genetic testing may identify a cause or associated genetic condition but does not replace clinical ASD diagnosis.**

## What if the child has lost skills?

If a child regularly used a skill previously and then stopped using it, report this separately to a clinician.

It is useful to clarify:

- which skill had been established
- how long the child used it
- when the change began
- whether the change was gradual or sudden
- whether only speech or also play, social and motor skills changed
- whether seizures or other medical changes occurred at the same time.

Regression can feature in the developmental history of some autistic children, but it must not automatically be attributed to autism without considering other explanations.

The pattern of regression may influence a clinician's decision about neurological or other additional assessments.

## Pain and other illnesses matter too

A child with ASD can have the same physical illnesses as any other child.

When a child has difficulty clearly communicating pain, a change in behaviour may be the first noticeable sign of a medical problem.

Sudden increases in irritability, sleep disturbance, refusal of food, aggression, self-injurious behaviour or avoidance of touch should not automatically be explained by autism itself.

Consider pain or other illness and seek medical assessment when necessary.

## Does every child need the same investigations?

No.

For one child, an adequate diagnostic picture may emerge from a psychiatrist's clinical assessment and developmental history.

Another child may also need a hearing assessment and psychological evaluation.

A third may require a neurologist and EEG.

For a fourth, there may be good reasons to consider genetic consultation and specialist investigations.

The investigation plan is determined by the individual child's clinical situation, not simply by the term "autism".

## Four questions for your doctor before an additional investigation

Before an expensive or complex investigation, consider asking:

1. **What specific medical question are we trying to answer?**
2. **What condition do you want to confirm or exclude?**
3. **How will the result change further assessment or treatment?**
4. **What would change if we did not undertake the investigation yet?**

These questions do not replace clinical judgement, but they can help the family understand each step.

## What not to arrange before the first appointment

Do not consider a universal paid package to be mandatory preparation for an initial diagnostic visit:

- ADOS-2
- ADI-R
- EEG
- MRI
- extensive laboratory tests
- a genetic panel
- whole-exome sequencing
- consultations with every possible specialist.

Bring results of any investigations already carried out for medical reasons.

It is generally better to choose a new investigation once the specific diagnostic or medical question is clear.

## Quick reference for parents

| Situation | What may be needed |
| --- | --- |
| **ASD is suspected** | Psychiatrist's diagnostic assessment |
| **Speech is delayed** | Speech and language assessment, and hearing assessment if indicated |
| **General development is delayed** | Psychological developmental assessment |
| **Seizures have occurred** | Neurological assessment, possibly EEG |
| **The child has lost skills** | Tell the doctor and evaluate possible causes of regression |
| **Other neurological signs are present** | Neurologist and further investigations when indicated |
| **Signs of an inherited condition are present** | Genetic consultation and appropriate testing |
| **Behaviour suddenly changes substantially** | Do not attribute everything to ASD, consider pain and physical illness |
| **MRI is offered solely to "confirm autism"** | Ask about the specific medical indication |

## Common misconceptions

### "A diagnosis without ADOS-2 is invalid"

No. Kazakhstan's clinical protocol lists ADOS-2 as a supplementary diagnostic method.

### "A normal EEG means there is no autism"

No. EEG answers a different medical question and does not itself confirm or rule out ASD.

### "MRI shows whether a child has autism"

No. Standard MRI is not an ASD diagnostic test.

### "Blood tests can show autism"

Routine laboratory tests do not establish an ASD diagnosis.

### "Genetic testing can replace a psychiatrist"

No. Genetic diagnosis may help identify a particular biological or inherited cause, but it does not replace clinical ASD diagnosis.

### "We must visit every specialist before seeing a psychiatrist"

The reviewed versions of the relevant documents do not establish such a universal mandatory pathway for every child.

## What to remember

The goal when autism is suspected is not to subject the child to as many tests as possible.

The main priority is a good-quality clinical diagnostic assessment, followed by additional investigations only where they answer a specific medical question.

**ADOS-2 and ADI-R** may supplement diagnosis.

**Psychological and speech assessments** help clarify a child's developmental profile.

**Hearing assessment** may be particularly important when there are speech difficulties.

**EEG, MRI and laboratory tests** address specific medical questions when indicated.

**Genetic assessment** may matter for some children but is not a universal prerequisite for confirming ASD.

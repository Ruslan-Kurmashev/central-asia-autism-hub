## Document profile

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Full title** | *National Guideline for the Assessment and Diagnosis of Autism in Australia* |
| **Developer** | Autism CRC, Australia |
| **Type** | National clinical guideline for professionals conducting assessment and diagnosis |
| **First edition** | 2018 |
| **Second edition** | 2023 update, with the second edition resource also listed in the catalogue as March 2024 |
| **Recommendations** | 66 consensus-based recommendations and Good Practice Points |
| **Approval** | National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC), 8 December 2023 |
| **Stated approval period** | Five years according to the official NHMRC statement on the guideline website |
| **Audience** | Professionals conducting clinical assessment of autism in children, adolescents and adults |
| **Primary source** | [Official Autism CRC resource](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/best-practice/assessment-and-diagnosis) |

**Important distinction:** NHMRC approved the document as a quality guideline, but it is not automatically binding Australian law, much less a regulatory instrument in Kazakhstan. The Autism CRC website specifically explains the voluntary nature of implementation.

Autism CRC issued the document under its former name. On 10 August 2026, the organisation announced its renaming to Sylvia Rodger Institute. The historical developer name and document details are retained. [Developer announcement](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/news/latest-news/were-evolving).

## 1. Purpose of the guideline

Unlike the document on supporting children after assessment, this guideline concerns **organising assessment and establishing a clinical diagnosis**. Its aim is to improve timeliness, accuracy, consistency and acceptability of assessment for the person.

The second edition retains the 2018 foundation, clarifies practical application and language, and considers neurodiversity values and individual preferences. The guideline applies across age groups and should not be misrepresented as a paediatric diagnostic protocol only.

## 2. Five parts of the professional pathway

The publicly accessible executive summary divides the process into the following areas:

| Section | Main professional question |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Guiding Principles | Are the person's rights, preferences, cultural and communication context respected? |
| 2. Foundations of Assessment | Are team competence, process quality and safe information sharing ensured? |
| 3. Making a Referral for Assessment | Are there grounds to discuss referral for assessment, and what does the person expect? |
| 4. Comprehensive Needs Assessment | What are the person's strengths, support needs, functioning and health characteristics? |
| 5. Diagnostic Evaluation | Do the clinical findings meet diagnostic criteria, and what needs to be explained afterwards? |

Recommendation 12 provides for referral to support at any stage when a relevant need is identified. Findings at each stage are explained to the person accessibly. Comprehensive assessment includes assessment of functioning and medical evaluation, with diagnostic evaluation conducted where indicated.

## 3. Comprehensive needs assessment is more than an autism test

*Comprehensive Needs Assessment* distinguishes two areas:

- **Assessment of Functioning** - everyday activities, participation, strengths, environmental accessibility barriers and support needs.
- **Medical Evaluation** - health, wellbeing and medical issues requiring specialist assessment.

Thus, findings on functioning and medical assessment must not be reduced to a single numerical result or brief screen. The scope of action depends on individual circumstances and professional qualifications.

## 4. Diagnostic evaluation and allocation of roles

The official summary describes two ways of organising the process:

1. **Lead Practitioner Diagnostic Evaluation** - assessment led by a practitioner who draws on appropriate sources and collaborates with other professionals when needed.
2. **Consensus Team Diagnostic Evaluation** - collective discussion and diagnostic decision-making by the team.

The choice depends on which option provides timely, high-quality assessment in the individual case. **A lead practitioner does not mean assessment without external information**, and a team approach does not mean that everyone needs the same number of consultations.

Recommendation 13 clarifies pathway selection. A lead practitioner approach is considered when sufficient information supports a decision with high confidence. Team evaluation is considered when developmental history is complex, characteristics are subtle, multiple diagnoses are possible or confidence remains insufficient after lead practitioner evaluation.

### In-person assessment and telehealth

Recommendation 18 allows remote consultations alongside in-person appointments, not instead of all in-person meetings. Good Practice Points provide for at least one in-person meeting during assessment and in-person medical evaluation, taking account of the need for physical examination. This does not authorise remote diagnosis in Kazakhstan. [Recommendations 12, 13 and 18](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/best-practice/sites/default/files/resources/Assessment_and_Diagnosis_Guideline.pdf).

The guideline refers to DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 diagnostic classifications. Use of a foreign classification or instrument must not be presented as independent legal authorisation for a practitioner to diagnose ASD in Kazakhstan.

## 5. Human rights, communication and cultural applicability

The second edition places greater emphasis on accessibility and respect for autistic people. Consider:

- preferred communication methods, including AAC where needed
- age, linguistic and cultural context
- strengths alongside difficulties
- informed participation of the person and family within applicable consent rules
- risk of unequal access to diagnosis in rural and underserved regions.

It is important to distinguish legally valid informed consent from willingness to participate (assent). In practice, explain what will happen, provide accessible ways to ask questions or request a break, and respond to discomfort. A legal representative's consent does not remove the need to consider the views of the person being assessed.

These are professional principles, not grounds for automatically applying Australian personal data collection or medical service procedures in Kazakhstan.

## 6. What was updated

Among the changes, the authors identify more contemporary and respectful terminology, greater flexibility, expanded practical guidance and the use of *Lead Practitioner Diagnostic Evaluation* instead of a previous name that could imply assessment by one clinician in isolation.

The second edition increased the number of guiding principles from 6 to 11. Instead of the previous four-level classification of consensus-based recommendations, GRADE is used: strong or conditional recommendation. A conditional recommendation requires consideration of the circumstances of application. [Developer explanation of changes](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/best-practice/assessment-and-diagnosis/second-edition/micro-learning-transcript).

**Evidence caveat:** the 66 recommendations are described as consensus-based. NHMRC approval establishes the quality of the development process, but does not mean that a high-quality randomised trial has been conducted for every individual recommendation.

## 7. Applicability in Kazakhstan

| Principle that can be adapted | What should not be transferred without national verification |
| --- | --- |
| Comprehensive assessment of medical and functional needs | Australian clinicians' authority to establish an official diagnosis |
| Respectful communication and personal participation | Australian legal rules on consent and information sharing |
| Team composition according to justified complexity | Australian payment, referral and service access arrangements |
| Documentation and explanation of findings | Requirements for specific diagnostic instruments without local verification |

Official legal and organisational documents in Kazakhstan are available in the [catalogue of Kazakhstan regulatory documents](../kazakhstan-documents/). For medical issues, separately check current national clinical protocols and professional authority.

## 8. How to access the full primary source

General information and summaries are available without registration. The developer offers free registration for full access to recommendations on the portal and additional documents. The HTML version reproduces the guideline content. A [PDF of the second edition](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/best-practice/sites/default/files/resources/Assessment_and_Diagnosis_Guideline.pdf) was also located. File availability may change. This update checked open sections and indexed PDF provisions, not all 66 recommendations.

Open the [guideline page](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/best-practice/assessment-and-diagnosis), then the *Recommendations* section. Register on the developer's website if necessary.

This review **is not a line-by-line verification of all 66 recommendations**. For practical application of a specific provision, practitioners must consult its full wording and explanations.

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] The first and second editions and date of NHMRC approval have been established.
- [ ] Needs assessment and diagnostic evaluation are distinguished.
- [ ] Functioning, health and personal preferences are considered.
- [ ] Team composition matches professional authority and complexity.
- [ ] No single test is used as independent confirmation of ASD.
- [ ] The full wording of the relevant recommendation has been checked in the primary source.
- [ ] The Australian pathway is not presented as a mandatory procedure in Kazakhstan.

**Scope limitation:** this material explains a specific foreign guideline. It does not establish a diagnosis, national standard or individual prescription.

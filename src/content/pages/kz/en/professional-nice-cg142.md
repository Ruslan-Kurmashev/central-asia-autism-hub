## 1. Why adults need a separate guideline

Diagnosis and support in adulthood have features that cannot be fully addressed in clinical documents for children. A person may have a long history of adaptations, previously unrecognised needs, experience of seeking psychiatric or psychological help for anxiety and depression, difficulties at work, in social situations or when accessing medical care.

CG142 considers **not only the diagnostic conclusion**, but also subsequent decisions: support for independence, mental health, interaction with services, social life and employment.

The recommendations assume that adults participate in decisions about their own assessment and support. Where necessary, communication with relatives, partners or other informants must not automatically replace the person's own consent and views.

## 2. When to consider diagnostic assessment

NICE recommends considering the combination of social interaction or communication characteristics with repetitive patterns of behaviour or restricted interests, and the impact of these characteristics on daily life and the person's needs ([1.2.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations)).

**AQ-10 is not a test that establishes a diagnosis.** In [recommendation 1.2.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations), NICE considers AQ-10 for adults with suspected autism **without moderate or severe intellectual disability**. For people who have reading difficulties, NICE provides for the questions to be read aloud. A score of 6 or above, or continuing clinical suspicion, may be grounds for offering comprehensive assessment. A negative result must not automatically close the question where there are reasonable clinical grounds.

For adults with moderate or severe intellectual disability, CG142 describes a different approach to initial consideration of characteristics ([1.2.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations)). Applying the same questionnaire mechanically to all groups does not follow the guideline's reasoning.

**In Kazakhstan**, before using AQ-10 or other tools, separately check whether an appropriate language version exists, its scope of use, practitioner training and local rules.

## 3. What comprehensive assessment includes

In [section 1.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations), NICE describes team assessment by competent practitioners, taking account of developmental history and current adult needs.

| Area | Practitioner's task |
| --- | --- |
| **Developmental history** | Reconstruct characteristics present since early life, taking account of potentially limited historical information |
| **Current communication** | Establish accessible and preferred communication methods and the need for adjustments |
| **Daily functioning** | Understand difficulties and strengths at home, in education, at work and in social situations |
| **Mental and physical health** | Assess possible co-occurring conditions that require separate support |
| **Sensory characteristics and environment** | Consider noise, lighting, unexpected events and the need for predictability |
| **Preferences and goals** | Agree the significance of assessment and subsequent support with the person |

Comprehensive assessment is not reducible to a questionnaire, observation or diagnostic interview in isolation. NICE treats formal tools as aids to professional judgement in complex cases, not substitutes for individual assessment.

[Point 1.2.11](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations) does not provide for routine use of biological or genetic tests or neuroimaging to establish autism. Point 1.2.17 provides for additional individual investigations where clinically indicated. This does not exclude medical investigation for other justified reasons.

Under points 1.2.12-1.2.15, assessment also covers risks of self-injury, violence or exploitation, deterioration and loss of necessary support. When risks are identified, prepare a risk reduction plan and, if necessary, a crisis plan specifying responsibilities, service contacts and environmental adjustments.

### Communicating findings

At the beginning of the process, explain **why assessment is being conducted and how the person would prefer to receive its findings**. NICE recommends individualising feedback and discussing the involvement of other people in communication where agreed or necessary ([1.2.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations)).

For the practitioner, this means preparing a clear written conclusion and understandable next steps in an accessible format, including alternative ways to convey information.

[Point 1.2.18](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations) provides for offering a follow-up appointment to all adults diagnosed with autism, even if further help is not currently required or the person has declined it. This is an opportunity to discuss questions and future needs, not an obligation to accept an intervention.

## 4. Support after diagnosis: beyond clinical procedures

CG142 considers support for **social participation, daily living skills and desired employment**. Support is selected according to the person's own goals, rather than a single list of measures for all adults.

For example, someone who struggles to organise a sequence of daily tasks may benefit from a structured programme to develop relevant skills. For another person, clear communication with an employer and adjustments to organisational conditions may be a priority. [Section 1.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations) discusses different psychosocial and daily living interventions.

Some recommendations concern groups of adults with a particular cognitive needs profile. It is therefore inappropriate to infer from a single provision that all adults require social behaviour training or that a programme must suppress a person's individual characteristics.

[Point 1.4.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations) does not recommend facilitated communication. It should be distinguished from accessible augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), in which the content of the message is determined by the person, not another participant. Supporting access to a communication tool must not replace authorship of the message.

### Work and employment support

NICE considers individual supported employment programmes for some adults experiencing difficulty obtaining or retaining employment ([section 1.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations)). This is not a guarantee of employment or a list of legally mandatory measures in Kazakhstan.

A realistic individual plan may include agreeing a communication format, clear written instructions, predictable changes to schedules and assessment of which adjustments actually help the employee.

## 5. Co-occurring conditions, safety and adverse effects

Anxiety, depression, pain, sleep disturbances and other problems should not automatically be attributed to autism. They may require **separate specialist assessment and care**.

When behaviour threatens the wellbeing of the person or others, CG142 suggests considering physical health, social conditions, the sensory environment, changes in routine and communication difficulties. Subsequent intervention should have a specific aim and be assessed in terms of benefit, quality of life and possible harm ([section 1.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations)).

NICE distinguishes medication for **co-occurring medical conditions** from attempts to modify **core autistic characteristics** using medication. Mentioning a drug in a clinical discussion does not mean it can be prescribed without relevant indications. This review is not intended for selecting medicines, doses or regimens.

## 6. Practical example: an adult seeks assessment after years of difficulties

**Situation:** an adult reports persistent difficulties in unstructured workplace meetings, sensory overload and marked exhaustion after social interaction. They have not previously been assessed for autism.

An inappropriate approach would be to establish a diagnosis using only a questionnaire or, conversely, refuse assessment because of high professional achievement.

A better-supported professional process:

1. Establish the person's own concerns and characteristics present at different stages of life.
2. Consider medical and mental health conditions that might influence the presentation.
3. Where justified, offer appropriate **comprehensive diagnostic assessment** within local rules.
4. Discuss communication accessibility and possible adjustments during assessment itself.
5. Regardless of diagnosis, consider support for everyday difficulties within the organisation's competence.
6. Agree an understandable way to communicate findings, goals and subsequent actions.

## 7. Checklist for applying CG142

- [ ] The age group falls within the scope of the document.
- [ ] The person's own concerns and goals have been considered.
- [ ] A screening questionnaire is not presented as a diagnostic conclusion.
- [ ] Required professional competencies and assessment stages have been identified.
- [ ] Possible co-occurring conditions and environmental factors have been checked.
- [ ] Support targets outcomes meaningful to the person rather than abstract normalisation.
- [ ] Necessary consent has been obtained and accessible communication ensured.
- [ ] Current local rules, service options and referral pathways have been checked.
- [ ] The timing and criteria for reviewing the decision have been established.

## Limitations of application in Kazakhstan

NICE CG142 does not determine the legal diagnostic pathway for adults in Kazakhstan, qualification requirements for particular practitioners or the availability of public services. When preparing a local protocol, separately check current health care, psychological practice and social support rules, rights to consent and data protection.

Recommendations on diagnostic instruments may also rely on versions current when CG142 was developed. The listed methods must not be presented as automatically validated in Russian or Kazakh or suitable for remote diagnosis.

## Main conclusion

NICE CG142 is useful for organising **accessible, comprehensive and person-centred assessment and support in adulthood**. The document is concerned not only with diagnostic criteria but also with participation in life, work, mental and physical health and the person's right to influence decisions.

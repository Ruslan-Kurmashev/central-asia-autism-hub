**Important:** a living guideline does not mean that the entire base text is automatically rewritten each year. Priority topics are reviewed separately and published as supplementary papers.

## 1. Purpose and scope

The guideline addresses people at different life stages, not only children. It brings together diagnostic assessment, education, mental and physical health, family support, employment and community participation.

Unlike clinical protocols on a single procedure, it provides a **broad national framework for professional practice** within the New Zealand system.

## 2. Structure of professional topics

| Area | What to consider |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment and diagnosis | Clinical context, access to assessment and subsequent communication |
| Health care | Co-occurring conditions, mental wellbeing and barriers to accessing care |
| Education | Accessible learning methods and environmental adaptations |
| Family and whānau | Participation in decisions and access to understandable information |
| Community and adulthood | Self-determination, participation, employment and transition support |
| Professional practice | Competence, collaboration and evidence for approaches |

The document does not prescribe one universal therapy or propose a cure for autism.

### How evidence is graded

Appendix 1 explains the guideline's own recommendation-grading system.

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A | Good, applicable and clinically relevant evidence |
| B | Generally valid research with limitations in volume, consistency or applicability |
| C | Expert opinion only |
| I | Insufficient evidence for a recommendation |
| GPP (tick in tables) | Good Practice Point |

These designations should not automatically be equated with GRADE. The edition date does not mean that the evidence search for every recommendation took place in 2022.

### Examples of diagnostic recommendations

| Point | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1.2.1 (B) | One practitioner may conduct the initial assessment of a child. Multidisciplinary assessment is recommended if concerns persist |
| 1.2.2 (B) | An experienced multidisciplinary team is preferred for young people and adults. If no team is available, a specifically trained and experienced clinician may conduct the assessment |
| 1.2.6 (C) | Consider validity, reliability and appropriateness when selecting tests and interpreting results |
| 1.2.10 (GPP) | Audiological assessment is recommended for children with suspected autism or other developmental concerns |
| 1.4.3 (C) | Develop an action plan if diagnosis remains uncertain |
| 1.4.4 (C) | The written report includes developmental history, strengths, difficulties, autistic characteristics, recommendations and information about available support |

When discussing diagnosis with older adolescents and adults, consider their wishes regarding family involvement, privacy and support needs (1.4.5). Information should be accessible in different languages (1.4.6). These provisions do not determine professional authority in Kazakhstan. [Full text, Part 1 and Appendix 1](https://www.whaikaha.govt.nz/assets/Autism-Guideline/Aotearoa-New-Zealand-Autism-Guideline-Third-Edition.pdf).

## 3. Involvement of autistic people and families

The official description stresses combining research, professional expertise and autistic people's lived experience. The approach focuses on strengths, autonomy and meaningful participation.

In practice, this means understandable communication, respect for consent and preferences, and attention to changing needs. Specific procedures must comply with the law of the country where services are provided.

## 4. Māori and Pacific cultural context

The guideline addresses the specific cultural needs of tangata whaitakiwātanga Māori and Pacific peoples. The official page recommends involving appropriate cultural guides where needed and adapting communication and service arrangements.

The **transferable principle** is to consider language, family context, cultural values and access to support. Māori-specific roles and arrangements cannot simply be copied into Kazakhstan as a ready-made national regulatory model.

## 5. How thematic updates work

Whaikaha publishes supplements on selected topics following review of new evidence and expert discussion.

| Year | Example supplement | Difference from the base edition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2023 | Parent-mediated approaches | Updated information on supporting children through parental involvement |
| 2024 | Gender in autism assessment | Clarifies professional diagnostic assessment |
| 2025 | Designing environments for autistic people | Addresses accessibility of the built environment |
| 2026 | Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) | Additional topic on accessible communication |

Before applying a specific statement, consult the full relevant chapter and the most recent thematic supplement.

[Official list of supplementary papers](https://www.whaikaha.govt.nz/resources/strategies-and-studies/guidelines/nz-autism-guideline).

## 6. How to use the guideline in Kazakhstan

| Useful principle | Requires national verification |
| --- | --- |

| Interprofessional coordination | Responsibilities of psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation services (PMPK), physicians and social services |
| Cultural and linguistic adaptation | Quality of Russian and Kazakh tools and materials |
| Updating the evidence | National protocols and current literature |

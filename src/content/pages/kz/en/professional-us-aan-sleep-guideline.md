## 1. What the guideline addresses

Children and adolescents with ASD may experience difficulty falling asleep, frequent night waking, early waking and other sleep disturbances. The AAN document assesses approaches to sleep problems rather than universal pharmacological treatment of autism.

The recommendations distinguish clinical assessment, family education and consideration of additional measures with attention to safety.

## 2. Assessing causes and co-occurring conditions

For sleep disturbances, AAN recommends considering conditions and medicines that may affect sleep. The original document discusses possible sleep apnoea, epilepsy, gastrointestinal symptoms, anxiety, ADHD and certain medicines, among other factors.

| What to assess | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Concerns and daily routines | Help clarify the specific problem and its consequences |
| Medical conditions | Sleep disturbance may relate to another cause requiring assessment |
| Medicines currently used | Some medicines may affect sleep onset and continuity |
| Sensory and home environment | Noise, lighting, discomfort and routine may substantially affect the situation |
| Impact on the child and family | Determines the importance of the problem and acceptability of possible measures |

**Not all sleep disturbances should be attributed to ASD alone.** Potentially serious causes require assessment by an appropriate physician.

## 3. First-line behavioural measures

AAN considers parent education, support for predictable routines and individually appropriate behavioural sleep strategies to be initial approaches. This does not mean applying the same programme to all children.

The original document describes different behavioural approaches, but their acceptability, risks, communication accessibility and the circumstances of individual families require separate assessment. **Ignoring a child's distress or using coercive methods must not be automatically recommended.**

High-quality studies of behavioural approaches were limited in this particular systematic review, so professional decisions require transparent discussion of expected benefits and uncertainty.

## 4. Discussion of melatonin and medical limitations

The guideline discusses possible use of melatonin after medical causes have been assessed and appropriate non-pharmacological measures have had insufficient effect. This is **not a recommendation to start medication independently**.

Important limitations:

- evidence differs by outcome: sleep onset, total sleep duration and night waking are not the same
- when prescribed by a physician, AAN recommends choosing pharmaceutical-grade melatonin if available, because the composition and purity of other products may vary
- adverse effects are possible, and reliable evidence on long-term safety is limited
- the US guideline does not determine regulatory status, indications or availability of medicines in Kazakhstan.

**Doses, administration times, medication changes or discontinuation are deliberately not specified here.** These matters are for a physician considering the individual circumstances and applicable legislation.

## 5. What is known about effectiveness

The 2020 systematic review identified studies reporting improvement in some sleep parameters. This does not mean that effects are identical across all outcomes and all children.

| Outcome | What to consider when interpreting it |
| --- | --- |
| Time taken to fall asleep | Changes in this measure do not equal improvement in all aspects of sleep |
| Sleep duration | Assessed separately from subjective satisfaction |
| Frequency and duration of waking | Effects may be uncertain and differ across studies |
| Adverse effects | Short-term tolerability does not establish long-term safety |

Therefore, **AAN Level B does not guarantee effectiveness** and does not justify prescribing without an individual professional decision.

**Weighted blankets and specialised mattresses.** AAN states that evidence is insufficient to support their routine use for disrupted sleep. The weighted blanket study reported no serious adverse events, but this does not establish effectiveness or mean the product is suitable for every child. Assess comfort, the ability to remove the blanket freely and individual risks. Do not present such a product as a treatment for insomnia.

### Assessing outcomes

A practical monitoring approach, not a separate validated AAN scale: agree with the family which outcomes to track before and after changing support. Examples include time taken to fall asleep, duration and number of night wakings, daytime wellbeing and adverse events. If the situation worsens or the expected benefit is absent, review the plan with the responsible practitioner. AAN does not recommend routine use of weighted blankets or specialised mattresses to improve sleep. This is not equivalent to prohibiting individual use after assessing safety and preferences.

## 6. Age, evidence and status

The 2020 publication relied on studies identified through December 2017. This is a substantial limitation when applying it in 2026. Practitioners should also assess newer studies and current national documents.

The guideline concerns the paediatric population, does not provide a standalone algorithm for adults and is not intended for parents to use as a personal treatment instruction.

## 8. Brief checklist

- [ ] Physical, neurological and mental health causes of sleep disturbances are considered.
- [ ] Medicines and other factors affecting sleep have been reviewed.
- [ ] Initial non-pharmacological approaches are discussed with attention to the child's interests and safety.
- [ ] Possible adverse effects of medical interventions are not ignored.
- [ ] No self-prescribing regimens from the foreign guideline are used.
- [ ] The systematic search ended in 2017 and this is taken into account.
- [ ] Decisions are based on applicable rules and professional assessment in Kazakhstan.

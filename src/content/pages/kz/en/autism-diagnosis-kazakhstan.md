If parents have concerns about their child's development, or screening indicates an increased likelihood of autism spectrum disorder (ASD), the next step is not to look for a single "autism blood test" but to obtain a comprehensive diagnostic assessment.

In Kazakhstan, medical diagnosis of ASD falls within the competence of a **psychiatrist**. Primary healthcare physicians have limited authority regarding certain borderline mental and behavioural disorders, but ASD is not included in that limited list. A paediatrician, general practitioner, psychologist, speech and language specialist, neurologist and other professionals can help identify developmental characteristics and conduct additional assessments. Their conclusions alone, however, do not replace a psychiatrist's medical diagnosis.

For a basic explanation of the terms, see [Screening, assessment and autism diagnosis: what is the difference?](../screening-assessment-and-autism-diagnosis/).

## The essentials in one minute

A possible pathway looks like this:

**parents or a professional notice differences -> primary healthcare or screening -> mental health service -> psychiatrist -> diagnostic assessment -> additional methods if needed -> diagnostic conclusion**

The sequence of appointments may vary. Not every child needs the same specialists, tests and investigations.

## Who does what?

| Professional or procedure | Main purpose | Can establish a medical ASD diagnosis? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psychiatrist | Conducts clinical psychiatric diagnosis | **Yes** |
| Paediatrician / general practitioner | Monitors development, identifies concerns and refers when appropriate | No |
| M-CHAT-R | Identifies increased likelihood of ASD in children of specified ages | No |
| Psychologist | Assesses development and psychological functions | No |
| Speech and language specialist | Assesses speech and communication | No |
| Neurologist | Assesses neurological conditions when indicated | No |
| ADOS-2 / ADI-R | Provide supplementary standardised diagnostic information | Not on their own |
| PMPK (psychological-medical-pedagogical consultation) | Addresses special educational needs and educational support | Does not replace medical diagnosis |

A medical diagnosis and a PMPK conclusion serve different purposes. Read [Psychiatrist, PMPK, VKK and MSE: who is responsible for what in Kazakhstan?](../psychiatrist-pmpk-vkk-mse-kazakhstan/).

## How the diagnostic process may begin

There are several possible starting points.

Parents may notice differences in interaction, speech, play or behaviour themselves. A paediatrician or another professional may raise concerns. In younger children, screening may identify an increased likelihood of autism.

No single characteristic automatically means autism. Delayed speech, not responding to a name or atypical eye contact need to be considered in the context of overall development, not treated as automatic proof of a diagnosis.

## What role does M-CHAT-R play?

The published version of Kazakhstan's Standard for the Organisation of Paediatric Care provides for the modified screening questionnaire M-CHAT-R. Its appendix states that the first screening is conducted between **16 and 30 months**.

Parents of children aged 16–30 months can [complete the first stage of M-CHAT-R on AutismHub](/kz/en/tools/mchat-r/). A score of 3–7 requires a separate Follow-Up interview with a professional. The questionnaire does not replace diagnostic assessment.

The standard's algorithm distinguishes three score ranges:

- 0-2 points: low likelihood
- 3-7 points: moderate likelihood, for which follow-up questions in the second stage and diagnostic evaluation are provided
- 8-20 points: high likelihood, for which proceeding directly to diagnostic assessment without second-stage questions is permitted.

The key distinction:

> **M-CHAT-R estimates likelihood and helps determine whether further assessment is needed. It does not diagnose autism.**

For further explanation, see [Screening, assessment and autism diagnosis: what is the difference?](../screening-assessment-and-autism-diagnosis/).

## Where to seek help in Kazakhstan

In the public healthcare system, the pathway often begins with primary healthcare. The published standard for mental healthcare states that when a mental or behavioural disorder is suspected but falls outside the primary care physician's authority, the patient is referred, according to their territorial registration, to:

- a mental health office (KPZ)
- a primary mental health centre (PTsPZ)
- a mental health centre (TsPZ).

The standard also covers non-governmental mental healthcare, including private healthcare organisations and private medical practice. Therefore, a family may approach a private medical organisation. For a medical diagnosis, the essential point is that the diagnostic decision is made by a psychiatrist acting within their professional competence.

### If the child is between 10 and 18 years old

Kazakhstan's Healthcare Code separately provides access to mental healthcare services for minors **aged 10 to 18 without a referral from a primary healthcare specialist**.

For younger children, the public-sector pathway may begin with primary healthcare. Confirm the booking and referral requirements with the service you choose.

## What happens at a psychiatrist's appointment?

ASD diagnosis should not depend on a single sign or one short observation.

Clinical ASD protocol No. 145 provides for assessment of the presenting symptoms and **developmental history**. The clinician considers the child's developmental history alongside the current clinical picture and diagnostic criteria.

### Developmental history

The psychiatrist may ask:

- when the family first noticed differences
- how speech and communication developed
- how the child uses gestures and other ways of communicating
- how the child plays
- how they interact with adults and other children
- whether repetitive actions or restricted interests are present
- how they respond to changes
- whether they acquired skills that were subsequently lost.

It is especially important to report **the loss of previously established skills**.

### Current clinical picture

The psychiatrist observes the child and considers the overall presentation. The available Kazakhstani clinical protocol No. 145 uses the ICD-10 F84 diagnostic category and examines the combination of differences in social interaction, communication and restricted or repetitive behaviour.

A statement such as "does not make eye contact", "does not speak" or "likes to spin objects" is therefore not sufficient evidence of a diagnosis by itself.

## How does the psychiatrist reach a decision?

The diagnostic decision draws on several sources:

**developmental history + clinical observation + information from the family and other professionals + diagnostic criteria**

The psychiatrist may also consider previous investigations and professional reports if they are relevant to the question.

A medical diagnosis should not be reduced to the result of:

- one questionnaire
- one score
- one observed behaviour
- a single non-medical professional's report
- EEG, MRI or a laboratory test.

## Is ADOS-2 mandatory?

Kazakhstani clinical protocol No. 145 lists **ADOS-2** and **ADI-R** as supplementary assessment methods and states that the treating clinician determines whether they are indicated.

There is therefore no basis for saying that every child must undergo ADOS-2 before a medical diagnosis can be made.

The reverse is equally important:

> **An ADOS-2 result is not, by itself, a medical ASD diagnosis.**

It may contribute to a more comprehensive assessment.

For other tests and investigations, see [What investigations may be needed when autism is suspected in Kazakhstan?](../tests-and-assessments-for-suspected-autism-in-kazakhstan/).

## Could additional investigations be needed?

Yes. Sometimes the clinician needs more information about development or to investigate a separate medical condition.

Depending on the circumstances, this could include:

- psychological assessment
- speech and communication assessment
- a hearing assessment
- neurological consultation
- other laboratory or instrumental investigations for specific medical indications.

This does not mean that every child with suspected ASD needs the same battery of tests.

EEG, MRI and blood tests **are not standalone autism tests**. If another investigation is proposed, it can help to ask:

> **What specific medical question will this investigation answer?**

See [What investigations may be needed when autism is suspected in Kazakhstan?](../tests-and-assessments-for-suspected-autism-in-kazakhstan/).

## What if no diagnosis is reached at the first appointment?

A first visit does not necessarily result in an immediate diagnosis.

Different outcomes are possible:

**ASD is diagnosed.** The psychiatrist explains the conclusion and the next recommendations.

**ASD is not confirmed.** This does not mean the child's observed difficulties are unimportant. Another explanation or support for specific needs may require consideration.

**There is not enough information yet.** The psychiatrist may seek further information, propose additional assessment or review development over time.

If assessment is ongoing, consider asking:

- what information is still missing
- why an additional investigation is needed
- when the next stage is planned
- how to address the child's present difficulties while the assessment continues.

## What happens after a diagnosis?

Following the diagnostic decision, information is entered into the medical record and the clinician explains the outcome and next steps.

A parent or legal representative has the right, under the applicable legislation, to receive information about their child's health, including examination results and diagnosis.

After the consultation, consider clarifying:

1. What diagnosis was made and what code is recorded?
2. What evidence supports this conclusion?
3. Are additional investigations needed and what questions will they answer?
4. What are the next medical steps?
5. Where can a record or extract be obtained if another professional or institution needs it?

Educational pathways, PMPK, VKK, MSE and further support are separate processes. Their differences are explained in [Psychiatrist, PMPK, VKK and MSE: who is responsible for what in Kazakhstan?](../psychiatrist-pmpk-vkk-mse-kazakhstan/).

## What to bring to a diagnostic appointment

There is no need to order the largest possible battery of tests in advance. It is more useful to bring information that helps explain your child's development.

If already available, you can bring:

- earlier medical reports
- psychological assessment results
- reports from speech and language specialists and other professionals
- hearing test results
- results of ADOS-2 or other diagnostic procedures already completed
- results of previously prescribed EEG, MRI or other tests
- a list of current medications
- a report from preschool or school, if available.

You can also write down:

- what specifically concerns you
- when you first noticed it
- how speech and communication developed
- how your child communicates needs
- how they play and interact with others
- whether previously established skills have been lost
- whether behaviour differs at home, preschool, school or elsewhere.

Specific examples are generally more informative than broad labels.

Instead of:

> "He cannot communicate at all."

A more useful description is:

> "When he wants a drink, he takes an adult by the hand and leads them to the fridge. He usually does not say the word or point at the bottle."

## What not to arrange yourself before the first appointment

Do not assume you must independently arrange the following as a prerequisite:

- EEG
- MRI
- genetic testing
- a large panel of laboratory tests
- ADOS-2 only because someone told you diagnosis is impossible without it.

If a test was already done for another reason, you can show the result to the psychiatrist. The clinical pathway does not establish a universal mandatory package of "autism investigations" for all children.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a paediatrician or general practitioner diagnose autism?

Paediatricians and general practitioners are important in monitoring development, identifying possible concerns and making referrals. Kazakhstan's Healthcare Code states that a psychiatrist establishes a diagnosis of a mental or behavioural disorder, while primary healthcare's authority regarding certain borderline disorders is defined separately.

### Can a neurologist make a medical ASD diagnosis?

A neurologist assesses neurological conditions and can contribute investigations when indicated. A psychiatrist makes the medical diagnosis of a mental or behavioural disorder.

### If ADOS-2 indicates ASD characteristics, is there already a diagnosis?

No. ADOS-2 is a supplementary method and must be considered with the rest of the diagnostic information.

### Is PMPK assessment required first?

PMPK and medical diagnosis have different roles. A PMPK report is not a mandatory condition for a psychiatrist to undertake a medical diagnostic assessment.

### Are EEG and MRI required to confirm autism?

Not as a universal condition. These investigations may be used for other medical questions when indicated.

## What to remember

Medical diagnosis of ASD in Kazakhstan is **a clinical process, not a single test**.

M-CHAT-R identifies increased likelihood. The psychiatrist reviews developmental history and the current clinical picture against diagnostic criteria. ADOS-2 and ADI-R may provide additional information when indicated. Other investigations address specific clinical questions rather than provide one universal "autism test".

If you are still trying to understand the terminology, start with [Screening, assessment and autism diagnosis: what is the difference?](../screening-assessment-and-autism-diagnosis/).

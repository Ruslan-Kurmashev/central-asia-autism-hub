After an autism assessment, it is important not only to receive the results but also to understand **what the clinician concluded and whether the diagnostic process is complete**. This article explains the documents and the next medical step. If an ASD diagnosis has already been established, the family's subsequent pathway is covered separately.

## 1. What result did you receive?

There are several possible outcomes. Do not decide whether a diagnosis has been established solely from test scores or a preliminary verbal impression.

| What the professional said | What this means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A medical ASD diagnosis has been established | A doctor has reached a diagnostic conclusion | Clarify the wording, recommendations and whether another appointment is needed. Then see the first-30-days guide |
| The conclusion is provisional or the available information is insufficient | Assessment is not finished and questions remain | Ask what information is missing, who will obtain it and when you will meet again |
| An ASD diagnosis has not been established following the assessment | The doctor did not establish ASD based on the available evidence | Ask how the original concerns are explained and whether further assessment or monitoring is appropriate |

If you only have a psychologist's report, ADOS-2 or ADI-R results, or a PMPK conclusion, ask separately whether there is a medical diagnostic report and who is responsible for the medical conclusion.

Even without an ASD diagnosis, concerns about speech, development, behaviour or the child's health may still need attention. The relevant qualified professional decides whether another evaluation is indicated.

## 2. How to read a medical report

Look for four elements in the document, or ask the doctor to explain them.

1. **Who issued the document?** Is it a medical report, a psychological assessment report or recommendations from another service? These documents have different purposes.
2. **What conclusion was reached?** Is a diagnosis established, described as provisional or still under assessment? If a code is given, ask what it means in the context of the report.
3. **What evidence supports the conclusion?** What developmental history, observations and assessment results were considered? Parents are not expected to interpret all medical terminology themselves.
4. **What happens next?** Is another consultation, test, period of observation or follow-up needed? Ask why and when.

One useful question is: "What medical conclusion was reached today, and exactly what should we do next?"

In Kazakhstan, patients and their legal representatives have the right to understandable information about health, assessment results and diagnosis. If the written wording is unclear, ask the clinician who issued it to explain.

## 3. How test scores differ from professional conclusions

| Document or method | What it provides | What cannot automatically be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A relevant doctor's medical report | A medical conclusion based on clinical assessment | That all children with the same diagnosis have the same needs |
| ADOS-2 and ADI-R | Structured information for diagnostic assessment when these tools are used | That one score or category independently confirms or rules out ASD |
| Psychologist's report | Description of assessed developmental, behavioural or functional characteristics | That it automatically replaces a medical diagnosis |
| PMPK conclusion | Assessment of special educational needs and recommendations within PMPK's remit | That it is equivalent to a doctor's medical diagnostic report |

ASD diagnosis rests on combined evidence, not a single test. This principle is also reflected in the international NICE CG128 guidance. That guideline helps explain comprehensive assessment but does not set procedural rules for Kazakhstan's medical organisations.

For the whole diagnostic process, see [How a child is diagnosed with autism in Kazakhstan](../how-autism-is-diagnosed-in-kazakhstan/). For the responsibilities of different bodies, see [Psychiatrist, PMPK, VKK and MSE](../psychiatrist-pmpk-vkk-mse-kazakhstan/).

## 4. What to do if the outcome is unclear or reports disagree

If the doctor says there is not yet enough information, an explicit plan for clarifying the assessment is more useful than arranging every available test yourself.

1. Note what is unclear: whether a diagnosis exists, what a test result means, or why two reports differ.
2. Contact the clinician responsible for the medical conclusion. Ask what information has been considered and what is still needed.
3. Clarify the next step: a follow-up appointment, observation, information from preschool or school, or a clinically indicated investigation. Record who will make the decision and when it will be revisited.
4. If important disagreements remain, discuss another consultation or a second professional opinion through an accessible medical pathway.

MRI, EEG, genetic tests and repeating ADOS-2 are not automatically required just because the current conclusion is uncertain. Additional investigations should address a specific clinical question.

If staff do not explain the outcome or there is difficulty obtaining information, you can contact the medical organisation's patient support and internal review service. It deals with service organisation and quality issues. It is not a body that automatically overturns a diagnosis.

## 5. What is the next step after assessment?

- **ASD has been diagnosed.** Keep the medical report and recommendations and clarify any required medical follow-up. Then use [The first 30 days after an autism diagnosis in Kazakhstan](../first-30-days-after-autism-diagnosis-in-kazakhstan/) to consider support, PMPK and family next steps.
- **A final conclusion has not yet been reached.** Obtain a clear plan for clarification and a date for the next contact. Do not treat an individual test result as a final diagnosis.
- **ASD has not been diagnosed.** Discuss what to do about the original developmental or health concerns and when further observation or consultation would be useful.

Before leaving, make sure you can answer three questions:

- What assessment outcome has been recorded at this time?
- Are there unresolved questions, and who is following them up?
- What should happen next, and when?

If the medical conclusion is still uncertain, this does not mean the child's everyday needs should be ignored. Communication and learning support, and other available measures, can be discussed with qualified professionals according to assessed needs without treating support as proof of a diagnosis.

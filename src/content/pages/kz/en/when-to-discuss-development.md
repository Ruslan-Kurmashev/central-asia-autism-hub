Parents do not need to be certain that their child is autistic or has another condition before asking questions about development. Persistent differences in communication, behaviour, learning or responses to the environment, or the loss of previously acquired skills, can all be reasons to speak with a professional.

One characteristic alone does not confirm autism. There can be different explanations for an observed behaviour. The aim of an initial conversation is not to reach an immediate diagnosis but to consider the child's development carefully and decide whether further assessment is needed.

## You do not need to wait until you are certain

Parents sometimes put off a conversation because they worry about being mistaken or feel that their observations are not serious enough. You do not need to know the cause before seeking advice.

NICE guidance says that parental concerns about a child's development or behaviour should be taken seriously, even if other people have not noticed the same differences. At the same time, any individual sign may have several possible explanations.

Discussing development does not mean that a diagnosis will automatically be made. The conversation is an opportunity to gather information, clarify observations and consider possible next steps.

## What observations can you discuss?

A conversation with a professional may be useful when parents repeatedly notice differences in one or more areas.

### Communication and interaction

For example, a child may:

- rarely respond when someone addresses them or calls their name
- find it difficult to use or understand gestures
- rarely show objects to share an interest
- take part in conversations or shared play in ways that differ noticeably from expectations
- find unwritten social rules difficult to understand
- need considerable effort to communicate.

### Behaviour, interests and changes

You might notice that a child:

- finds unexpected changes especially difficult
- repeats particular actions or movements
- uses objects in a distinctive and highly consistent way
- has particularly intense or narrowly focused interests
- relies strongly on a predictable sequence of activities.

### Sensory responses

Some children have unusual responses to:

- sounds
- light
- touch
- smells
- tastes or food textures
- pain, temperature or movement.

Such responses may affect sleep, eating, learning, visits to public places or everyday activities.

These examples are not a diagnostic checklist. Not every autistic child shows all of these characteristics, and some occur in children who are not autistic. The World Health Organization emphasises that autistic people's abilities, characteristics and support needs vary considerably and can change over time.

## Consider the overall pattern of observations

When discussing development, it helps to consider:

- how long a difference has been present
- how often it occurs
- whether it is noticeable in different situations
- what happens at home, preschool or school
- whether it affects communication, learning or everyday life
- whether it causes substantial distress for the child
- which conditions help the child feel more settled and confident.

Some differences become noticeable only under high demands, in unfamiliar places or when routines change. In other settings, a child may manage more comfortably because the environment and support are familiar.

The absence of obvious difficulties in one setting does not always mean there are no developmental concerns. NICE recommends considering signs in the context of the child's overall development, different settings and the effects on the child and family.

## Loss of previously acquired skills

Particular attention is needed when a child stops using a skill that they previously used.

For example, a child may:

- stop saying words they used before
- interact noticeably less with others
- lose previously established social skills
- lose motor skills.

Do not try to explain such changes on the basis of online information alone. Discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional without a prolonged delay. Medical advice is particularly important when motor skills are lost or weakness or a noticeable change in walking appears. These signs require assessment for possible neurological and other causes rather than an assumption that they are related only to autism.

## How to prepare for a conversation

To make the discussion specific, you can write down a few observations beforehand:

- exactly what you noticed
- when it began
- how often it happens
- which situations make it most noticeable
- how it affects the child and family
- what helps your child
- what other adults who know the child well have observed.

Specific situations are more useful than broad labels.

For example, rather than saying, "He always ignores people," you could say, "At home he often does not turn when his name is called, although he responds to some familiar sounds."

It is also important to talk about the child's strengths, interests, preferred ways of communicating and the situations that make shared activities easier.

## What might happen next?

A professional may:

- ask about the child's developmental history in more detail
- clarify which situations bring out the differences
- consider other possible explanations
- decide whether further monitoring or investigations are appropriate
- refer the child for a more detailed assessment when needed.

The specific pathway depends on the healthcare system, region and professionals available. This article does not prescribe one universal pathway.

## Questionnaires and online tests

**M-CHAT-R developmental screening.** Parents of children aged 16-30 months can [complete the 20-question first stage in English](/kz/en/tools/mchat-r/). Some scores require a further Follow-Up interview with a professional. Screening does not diagnose or rule out autism.

A questionnaire may help organise observations or prepare questions for a professional, but its result is not a diagnosis.

A high score does not confirm autism, and a low score does not rule it out. NICE states that identification and diagnostic instruments should not be used on their own to confirm or exclude autism.

## What to remember

Parents do not need to identify the cause of developmental differences themselves. Noticing persistent changes or difficulties and describing them in concrete terms is enough to start a discussion.

Asking for professional advice does not mean that a diagnosis has already been made. It is a way to understand the child's development better, consider possible explanations and decide whether further assessment is needed.

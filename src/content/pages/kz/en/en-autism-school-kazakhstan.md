## In brief

When planning a child's schooling, distinguish three questions: **how the child is enrolled**, **which special educational needs they have**, and **what adjustments or additional support they require**.

These are related but separate matters. They should not be determined automatically by an autism diagnosis alone.

## Starting Grade 1 in the 2026-2027 academic year

According to the [official gov.kz guidance](https://www.gov.kz/situations/15/80?lang=ru), applications for Grade 1 for the **2026-2027 academic year** were accepted from **27 May to 31 August 2026**, with a phased start in different regions. Parents and legal representatives could submit applications through eGov.kz or directly through a school office. These dates apply to **that academic year only** and should not be assumed to apply to future years. The [official English-language admission page](https://www.gov.kz/situations/15/intro?lang=en) is also available.

Under the admission rules, children who are six years old by 1 September are enrolled regardless of their level of academic preparation. Where a child turns six **after** 1 September, admission may take place at the request of their parent or legal representative. Other enrolment situations and transfers have their own procedures and should be checked separately.

Official guidance states that a school should not require documents beyond the prescribed list. If you are asked to meet an additional condition, request a reference to the specific rule and clarify the matter with the local education authority.

## Admission and educational support are not the same thing

Admission rules establish how a child enters school. A separate process may then be needed to determine individual educational adjustments and psychological and pedagogical support.

It is useful for the family to prepare examples of **what helps the child learn, communicate and participate** before meeting school staff.

## What is the role of PMPK?

**PMPK**, Kazakhstan's psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation service, can identify special educational needs and recommend relevant support.

Read the PMPK conclusion in practical terms: what educational needs are described, which conditions are recommended and how long the conclusion is valid.

A PMPK assessment is not a substitute for a medical autism diagnosis.

## What does the school support service do?

Rules governing the school-based **psychological and pedagogical support service** were approved by **Order No. 92 of 29 April 2025** and amended by **Order No. 144-NQ of 29 May 2026**.

The earlier **Order No. 6 of 12 January 2022** is no longer in force and should not be presented as the applicable set of rules.

The current rules address identifying difficulties, assessing individual needs, organising support and working with families. Specific arrangements should be based on a child's **educational needs**, not just the diagnostic label.

In practice, the school should plan support around what helps the child take part in learning.

## What adjustments can families discuss?

Depending on the child, adjustments might include:

- a predictable visual structure and timetable;
- accessible communication, including **augmentative and alternative communication (AAC)**;
- presenting instructions in a different way;
- extra time;
- sensory adjustments;
- support when moving between lessons;
- support during breaks and school activities;
- work towards clear learning and functional goals.

**Not every child needs every adjustment.** The aim is to agree on measures that address observed barriers to participation.

## If the school refuses admission or does not organise support

Ask for a **written explanation** of the decision and the relevant legal basis. Keep copies of your application, the school's responses and any PMPK recommendations.

If the issue cannot be resolved directly with the school, contact your **local education authority** or use an official government complaints channel.

Cases should be assessed individually. An organisational difficulty is not automatically the same as an unlawful refusal to enrol, but an unexplained verbal refusal should not be the only response a family receives.

## Home education is a separate question

**Education at home for medical reasons is not an automatic consequence of autism.**

Under the applicable framework, medical grounds for home education are linked to a conclusion from a **medical advisory commission (VKK)**. PMPK, by contrast, addresses the educational needs and recommendations.

These professional roles should not be confused.

## Questions to ask the school

1. Who is part of the psychological and pedagogical support team?
2. Who is the family's main contact?
3. Which special educational needs have been identified?
4. Which adjustments can the school actually provide?
5. How will measurable goals be formulated?
6. How often will support be reviewed?
7. What is the school's response to sensory overload, bullying, leaving the classroom unexpectedly or other risks?
8. How can the child communicate pain, anxiety or a need for a break?

## Your next step

Before meeting school staff, prepare a short **child profile**: strengths, preferred communication methods, main difficulties, sensory needs, safety considerations and **three to five conditions** that help the child learn and take part.

If you are still considering preschool, see [Autism and kindergarten in Kazakhstan: enrolment and support](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/en/help-kazakhstan/autism-kindergarten-support-kazakhstan/).

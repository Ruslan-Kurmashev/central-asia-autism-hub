## Who is a special educator or defectologist?

If a child has difficulty understanding learning tasks, using school materials, communicating or participating in lessons, a special educator can help identify an accessible way to learn. Their work primarily concerns education and the development of functional skills. It is not a separate treatment for autism.

In Kazakhstan, parents encounter titles such as special educator, teacher-defectologist, defectologist, specialist in teaching people with intellectual disabilities and corrective educator. The first three overlap: teacher-defectologist belongs to the professional group of special educators. The name of a service alone does not explain what or how the child will be taught.

## Requirements for specialists in Kazakhstan

[Order No. 31 of 24 February 2025](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/G25HP000031) establishes professional standards for educators in educational organisations. Special educators include, among others, teacher-defectologists, defectologists, speech therapy teachers, speech therapists, educators specialising in intellectual disabilities, hearing impairment and visual impairment. The standard describes professional functions and qualification levels. It also provides for an educator working with children who experience difficulties in communication and social interaction, including children with ASD.

Check the requirements for the particular position separately in the current version of [Order No. 338](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V090005750_). Professional standards and qualification descriptions serve different purposes. A certificate from any short course cannot be treated as equivalent to relevant professional education or retraining provided for by the rules. Nor can a single length-of-experience requirement be established for every position and category.

Ask the specialist to explain their training, the branch of special education they studied and the educational needs they have worked with. If the specialist provides medical services, the grounds for such activity must be checked separately. Educational qualifications alone do not confer the powers of a physician.

## Where and how to obtain educational support

You can begin by talking to the teacher, preschool educator or the psychological and pedagogical support service (Russian abbreviation SPPS) of the educational organisation. Describe specific difficulties and the conditions in which they arise. For example, a child understands a task in a quiet setting but cannot follow the instruction in a noisy classroom.

[Order No. 92 of 29 April 2025](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2500036047), taking account of the [2026 amendment](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2600038856), regulates the support service. The [rules and programmes for assessing special educational needs (SEN)](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2200026618), taking account of the [2025 amendments](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2500036082), provide for an in-depth assessment by the service's specialists with the consent of parents or legal representatives.

The psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation service (PMPK, Russian abbreviation) assesses educational needs and recommends necessary conditions and types of support. The psychological and pedagogical correction office (KPPK, Russian abbreviation) provides specialised psychological and pedagogical assistance. Under the [standard rules, Order No. 385](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2200029329), special educational organisations work on needs assessment, developmental programmes and education. Under the [special support standards](https://old.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2400034117), admission of children with disabilities to KPPK and several other special organisations requires the PMPK conclusion and recommendations and the consent of the legal representative.

![Possible pathways: contact the school or preschool, PMPK if necessary, support in an educational organisation, KPPK or another organisation, assessment and programme review.](/central-asia-autism-hub/images/editorial/diagrams/special-pedagog-kz-route-en.svg)

This diagram illustrates possible pathways, not a mandatory sequence for every family. School support, KPPK assistance and private sessions can complement one another. Check admission requirements, documents, availability and funding for a particular service with the organisation and local education authority. Do not postpone accessible educational adaptations solely because you are awaiting a medical conclusion.

Educational assessment does not replace medical diagnosis of ASD. Seek an appropriate healthcare professional if there is pain, sudden loss of previously acquired skills or new substantial difficulties.

## What does a special educator work on?

Work may include assessing educational needs, adapting tasks, teaching reading and mathematics, using communication aids, developing independence in learning and collaborating with the family. Not every specialist is equally competent in all areas.

| Difficulty | Possible educational goal |
| --- | --- |
| Difficulty starting a task | Locate necessary materials using an accessible list. |
| Difficulty reading text | Learn a specific component of reading at an appropriate level. |
| Unclear meaning of numbers | Match the quantity of familiar objects to a number. |
| Constant reminders are needed | Use an understandable sequence of actions with less unnecessary assistance. |
| Difficulty asking for help | Ask for help through an accessible method, including AAC. |
| Noise interferes with learning | Find conditions in which participation is accessible. |

Choose a goal according to the child's actual needs, not just the specialist's list of exercises. Consider interests, opportunities to make choices, language of instruction and familiar methods of communication.

## How specialists differ

| Specialist | Main focus |
| --- | --- |
| Special educator / defectologist | Teaching and adapting educational tasks. |
| Speech and language therapist | Speech, language and communication within their qualifications. |
| Teaching assistant | Supporting participation in education. |
| Occupational therapist | Participation in everyday activities, adapting activities and environments. |
| Psychologist | Psychological assessment and support within their training. |
| Physician | Medical diagnosis and care within their specialty. |

These are guides to allocating tasks, not an exhaustive list of legal powers. Professions may overlap. Applied behaviour analysis (ABA) is a field of behaviour analysis and related interventions. The label ABA does not replace checking a person's education, professional role and specific programme.

## Teaching methods and their evidence base

Choose a method after identifying the reason for the difficulty. If a child cannot follow an instruction because the format is inaccessible or there is sensory discomfort, repeating the instruction will not resolve the underlying problem.

| Method | Example of use |
| --- | --- |
| Direct instruction | Demonstrate a new learning step, offer practice and understandable feedback. |
| Breaking a task into steps | Divide preparation of the workspace into accessible actions. |
| Prompting | Help the child start or complete a difficult step, gradually reducing unnecessary assistance. |
| Modelling and video modelling | Show an example of an action live or in a recording. |
| Visual supports and self-management | Use a schedule or list that the child can manage. |
| Communication support | Make choosing, reporting a difficulty, refusing and requesting help accessible. |

In the [review by Hume and colleagues (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33449225/), these and other individual practices are part of the research base on supporting autistic children and young people. This classification does not mean that every procedure is suitable for every skill. An educator's diploma also does not establish the effectiveness of their entire programme.

If the child uses augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), coordinate its use during sessions and in the classroom with specialists. Do not require spoken language to be mastered first or restrict access to AAC to obtain correct answers. Do not guide the child's hand to select a message on their behalf.

## How an individual programme should be developed

Before teaching begins, the specialist needs to identify what the child can already do, where help is needed, which conditions facilitate participation and which barriers exist. The family and child take part in selecting priorities in an accessible way. Then agree on a specific goal, teaching method, necessary adaptations, way of observing progress and a date for discussing the result.

The individual developmental programme in a KPPK and the school curriculum have their own organisational requirements. The example below is intended for discussing an educational goal and does not replace the document prescribed by the organisation.

There is no universal number of sessions that guarantees mastery of a skill. First determine whether the teaching method helps and whether the result transfers to familiar settings. If there is no benefit, change the method or environment rather than automatically increasing demands.

## Example of an educational goal and outcome assessment

A child needs to prepare materials for a familiar task. With the specialist, the family selects five actions: find a notebook, a pencil, the relevant page, arrange the materials and start the first accessible item. Change the order or form of an action if necessary.

| Part of the plan | Illustrative example |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | Two of five steps were completed without additional adult assistance. |
| Goal | Use an accessible list and prepare materials with fewer unnecessary reminders. |
| Method | Demonstration, breaking the task into steps, reducing unnecessary prompts. |
| Support | Visual list, comfortable workspace, access to AAC and assistance. |
| Observation | Record independent steps, additional prompts, comfort and requests for help separately. |
| Review | Check how convenient the list is in the classroom and when tasks change, and adjust the plan if necessary. |

The numbers are fictional. They are not developmental norms, a mandatory percentage of independent actions or a promise of results. Independent use of the list may count as performance with a permanent adaptation. Asking for help or declining should not be considered inappropriate behaviour.

## How to choose a specialist

At the first consultation, ask to see documents confirming relevant education and to hear about experience with the chosen task. Find out how the baseline assessment is conducted, why a particular method is proposed, how changes are recorded and when the programme will be reviewed.

Clarify how the child can request help, ask for a break or decline an exercise. Agree on what information will be shared with the school and other specialists. If photographs or video recordings are planned, discuss the purposes, access and deletion date. Assessing a session usually does not require sharing identifiable materials in group chats.

At a private organisation, it is also helpful to clarify the content and cost of the service, payment arrangements and cancellation terms in advance. Do not treat the number of purchased sessions as a measure of quality.

## Red flags and safety

Promises to cure autism, guarantees of results after a fixed number of sessions and inability to explain the goal or method are reasons to reconsider the choice of specialist. Exercise particular caution with practices based on coercion, shame, suppression of safe stimming, mandatory eye contact or restriction of communication.

A child must not be deprived of water, food, access to the toilet, necessary rest or communication aids to complete a task. Do not restrain the child's hands or body to obtain a correct answer. If the child experiences pain or significant discomfort, or declines a learning attempt, stop and identify the reason. Safety in the face of an immediate threat of injury takes priority over teaching.

Assessing a specialist is not simply a matter of adding up checklist marks. Even one serious safety violation may be sufficient reason to stop the session and seek other help.

## What scientific research says

[Watkins and colleagues (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30869925/) analysed intervention research in inclusive educational settings. The authors reported predominantly positive social and communication outcomes. These findings relate to the methods and outcomes studied, not to every special educational service.

[Perrelet and colleagues (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613251352223) included 233 empirical studies. These comprised 90 single-case experimental studies, 28 randomised controlled trials and studies with other designs, including qualitative research. All 233 studies cannot be called experiments.

![Number of studies by selected outcomes: social skills 71, interaction 56, cognitive, motor and sensory skills 45, social environment 40, health and well-being 15, organisational factors 12, school climate 11, emotional regulation 11.](/central-asia-autism-hub/images/editorial/diagrams/special-pedagog-inclusion-outcomes-en.svg)

Counts for eight selected categories: social skills 71, social interaction 56, cognitive, motor and sensory skills 45, social environment 40, health and well-being 15, organisational factors 12, school climate 11, emotional regulation 11. Source: Perrelet et al., Autism, 2025, DOI 10.1177/13623613251352223. Categories overlap and do not cover all outcomes in the review. Frequency of research does not indicate effectiveness. Programme implementation was not assessed in every study.

[Tsou and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41868250/) (online 2024, issue 2026) reviewed 56 studies of social inclusion in schools. Greater access to participation did not necessarily lead to improved reciprocal relationships or friendships. A [2023 AJOT systematic review brief](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37585656/) also addresses participation in educational settings. It is helpful for families to discuss not only completing exercises but also access to lessons, communication, comfort and the ability to influence one's own participation.

Academic skills also deserve attention. A [meta-analysis of mathematics instruction (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1177/215416472405900303) examines several teaching methods, including different ways of presenting material. A [review of reading interventions (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.reia.2025.202745) covered 85 studies. Reading tasks differ, so support should be selected for a specific skill, not the general name of a method.

A [review of educational outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70015) (online 2025) includes 112 publications from 2012-2023. Academic outcomes were studied more often than attendance and disciplinary outcomes. A [2026 review and meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/rev3.70127) covers various categories of learners with special educational needs. Its overall results cannot be interpreted as a separate estimate of effect for autistic children. None of these reviews establishes the overall effectiveness of the profession of defectologist.

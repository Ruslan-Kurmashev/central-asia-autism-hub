## 1. How this document differs from diagnostic guidance

The new 2026 document concerns **support, interventions and life pathways**, not establishing an initial diagnosis. It updates the 2012 recommendations. The separate HAS 2018 guideline concerns detection of signs and diagnostic assessment.

Transferring conclusions about support into diagnostic criteria would be an error. Screening, diagnosis, support planning and outcome monitoring are distinct professional tasks.

## 2. Comprehensive assessment of functioning

HAS describes regular, multidimensional and interprofessional assessment based on observations of the child, family and professionals.

| Domain | What to consider |
| --- | --- |
| Communication | Accessible communication methods, comprehension, participation and AAC needs |
| Health | Sleep, nutrition, pain, epilepsy and other possible co-occurring conditions |
| Everyday life | Independence, learning, rest and environmental accessibility |
| Family | Priorities, resources, information and support needs |
| Environment | Adaptations to spaces, information, activities and social situations |

Assessment should not be reduced to one screening score or attempts to eliminate autism.

In section 3.1.2, HAS recommends standardised functional assessment at least annually, more often in early childhood and at significant transitions. Between assessments, the team monitors progress towards individual goals, acceptability of support and adverse changes.

## 3. Support and communication

HAS considers early individualised developmental and behavioural approaches, taking account of functional needs. Specific methods should be selected using current evidence, practitioner qualifications and the child's goals.

It is particularly important that **accessible communication support is included in the individual plan**, with AAC introduced when indicated without artificial prerequisites. This concerns accessibility of communication, not coercion into a specific way of expressing oneself.

### Early support: intensity and choice of approach

In section 3.1.1, HAS recommends offering infants and children with confirmed ASD risk or diagnosis at least 10 hours of interventions per week, delivered by professionals trained in developmental and behavioural approaches and parent support. Activities in everyday life involving families and educational professionals are also considered. This is an HAS recommendation for early support, not a universal standard for all ages or an individual prescription. The amount, goals and acceptability of support are assessed for the particular child. Specifying a number of hours does not itself demonstrate that one intensity is superior for every child.

For children under six, HAS identifies early intensive behavioural interventions (EIBI), naturalistic developmental behavioural interventions (NDBI), developmental approaches and parent-mediated interventions (PMI). Selection must consider evidence for the particular goal, accessibility of support, demands and the child's preferences.

### Interventions HAS does not recommend

Section 3.1.3 distinguishes the grounds for negative recommendations:

| Ground stated by HAS | Methods |
| --- | --- |
| Absence of studies | Doman-Delacato, Feuerstein, Padovan, facilitated communication, RPM, Saccade |
| Insufficient evidence | Primitive reflex integration, Son-Rise, 3i, neurofeedback, psychoanalytic approaches, Snoezelen |
| Studies did not demonstrate an effect | Auditory integration, including Tomatis, packing |

This is the guideline authors' classification of interventions for ASD, not an independent AutismHub systematic review. Absence of studies is not evidence of absence of effect. Therapy that is not recommended must be distinguished from ordinary accessible sensory activities chosen by the child for enjoyment or comfort. Facilitated communication should be distinguished from AAC in which the person remains the author of the message.

[HAS original, section 3.1](https://www.has-sante.fr/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-02/reco463_recommandation_tsa_mel.pdf).

## 4. Family involvement and quality of life

The recommendations emphasise parent education, counselling and support, peer support and access to respite for caregivers. Families are regarded as partners of the team, not passive recipients of prescriptions.

Professional goals should be meaningful to the child and family, with outcomes reviewed regularly. There is no basis for assuming that the same intensity and format of support suit every child.

## 5. Education, health and life transitions

HAS pays attention to inclusive educational environments and communication between institutions. Continuity, information sharing and the adolescent's participation in decisions are important during transition to adult services.

Medical symptoms require separate assessment, and medicines do not treat the core characteristics of autism. Pharmacological treatment of co-occurring conditions is a matter for qualified physicians and current clinical guidelines.

### Additional areas of support

| Area | Professional reference |
| --- | --- |
| Movement and leisure | Accessible physical activity in the individual plan, accounting for interests and health |
| Adolescence | Mental health support and preparation for adult services |
| High support needs | Arranging continuous intensive support where needed |
| Safeguarding | Assessment of pain, health, communication and environmental circumstances when behaviour is dangerous |
| Relationships and sexuality education | Accessible education on personal boundaries, relationships and safety |
| Supervision | Practitioner training, regular discussion of practice and team support |
| Child protection | Coordinated work between services accounting for child and family needs |

HAS uses the term profound autism to describe people with intensive continuous support needs. It is not an independent DSM or ICD diagnostic category or a synonym for dangerous behaviour. It must not be used to restrict access to education, health care or participation in life.

### How to interpret evidence levels

HAS explains that provisions without a separately specified grade are based on expert consensus. The strength of individual recommendations differs. The document's status does not mean that every approach has been shown effective by research of equal quality. For more precise reading, consult the [official English translation](https://www.has-sante.fr/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-10/dir3/reco463_recommandation_tsa_en.pdf) or French original.

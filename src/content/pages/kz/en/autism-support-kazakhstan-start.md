> **Regulatory sources checked on 9 October 2026.** This guide uses Kazakhstan's official legal and government sources. If a law or online service changes, the guidance must be updated.

When a family has questions about a child's development, suspects autism, or already has a diagnosis, it can be difficult to distinguish between a polyclinic, psychiatric services, PMPK, preschool or school, a medical advisory commission, disability assessment, social protection and private centres.

You do not need to navigate all these systems at once. **They serve different purposes.** Begin with the question that matters most right now.

## At a glance: which service deals with which question?

| What the family needs | A possible starting point | What the pathway addresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Questions about development, speech, behaviour, movement, sleep or other health concerns | The child's registered primary healthcare provider | Medical assessment, developmental monitoring, early intervention and referral when indicated |
| A medical assessment for autism spectrum disorder (ASD) | The healthcare system and, when appropriate, mental health services | Clinical assessment and medical diagnosis |
| Identification of special educational needs | PMPK | Psychological, medical and pedagogical assessment, with recommendations for educational support |
| Specialised psychological and educational support | PMPK, followed by an appropriate education or specialist organisation | Multidisciplinary assessment, an individual development programme, developmental sessions and consultation |
| Consideration of disability status | Healthcare provider / VKK, followed by MSE | Assessment of disability criteria and possible social protection measures |
| Social services or other support measures | Social protection services and relevant government portals | Eligibility-based services, benefits and provisions in an individual programme |
| A private specialist or centre | Check the precise service being offered | Licensing if required, qualifications, contract terms, costs, safety and evidence |

This table is a guide, **not a mandatory sequence for every family**.

## 1. If you are concerned about your child's development

For health and development questions, one starting point is the child's registered **primary healthcare provider** (known in Kazakhstan as *PMSP*, `ПМСП`).

The current paediatric care standard provides for **child development and early intervention centres** within primary healthcare organisations serving registered children. Their facilities include child-development services and audiological and psychophysical screening.

The standard also provides for multidisciplinary teams. Children with newly identified psychophysical developmental differences may be referred to such a team. An individual early intervention programme may be developed, with periodic assessment of how support is working.

Actual access to a centre or a particular professional can vary by region. A useful question for your polyclinic is:

**"Where does this organisation refer children for developmental assessment and early intervention?"**

### What to prepare

You do not need to decide on a diagnosis yourself. Write down specific observations, such as:

- how your child communicates needs, pain or requests for help;
- how they understand spoken language;
- how they use words, gestures, augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), or other means of communication;
- how they play and interact with others;
- how they cope with changes;
- how they eat and sleep;
- whether there have been unusual episodes involving movement;
- whether any previously used skills have been lost.

If your child's condition changes **suddenly**, new medical symptoms appear or there is an immediate risk to health or safety, seek medical care appropriate to the urgency rather than waiting for a routine PMPK appointment.

See also: [Where to seek help if you are concerned about your child's development in Kazakhstan](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/en/help-kazakhstan/development-concerns-kazakhstan/).

## 2. If you need a medical autism diagnosis

A medical diagnosis of ASD belongs within the healthcare system. PMPK, a teacher, a psychologist, a speech and language professional, or a private learning centre **cannot substitute for a medical diagnosis**.

Kazakhstan's mental health care standard provides for primary healthcare to identify possible mental or behavioural disorders and refer patients to specialist mental health services when the concern is beyond the primary healthcare professional's remit.

For ASD, the medical diagnostic decision falls within the professional competence of a psychiatrist.

For the detailed diagnostic pathway, see the [Russian-language guide to how an autism diagnosis is made in Kazakhstan](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/ru/parents/kak-stavyat-diagnoz-autizma-v-kazakhstane/).

To learn which tests may be relevant and which are **not routinely required** for autism diagnosis, see the [Russian-language guide to investigations when autism is suspected](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/ru/parents/obsledovaniya-pri-podozrenii-na-autizm/).

## 3. If you need preschool, school or educational support

This is a different pathway involving **PMPK**, Kazakhstan's *psychological, medical and pedagogical consultation service*.

PMPK carries out psychological, medical and pedagogical assessments and offers guidance related to special educational needs and support. **It should not be treated as an alternative to a medical autism diagnosis.**

### What changed in 2026?

Rules for the PMPK state service were amended by Order No. **180-NҚ dated 26 June 2026**. The changes took effect in July 2026.

Under the applicable rules:

- PMPK provides the service;
- applications may be submitted through the PMPK office or the digital government portal;
- the service is free;
- the waiting period for an assessment is **up to 30 calendar days**;
- for in-person applications, an assessment date is assigned on the day of application;
- for online applications, a date is assigned within **two working days**;
- a written conclusion is issued on the assessment day and/or delivered electronically to the applicant's portal account within **two working days**.

The rules also prohibit asking applicants to supply information that the authority can retrieve through government digital systems.

Check the current service information on gov.kz before applying. For the distinction between a psychiatrist, PMPK, VKK and MSE, see the [Russian-language explanation of their respective roles](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/ru/parents/psihiatr-pmpk-vkk-mse-kazakhstan/).

## 4. What may happen after a PMPK assessment?

A PMPK conclusion may provide the basis for specialised psychological and educational support.

The 2025 standard explicitly includes children with communication and social interaction difficulties, **including autism spectrum disorder**, among those who may receive such support.

The standard describes four main components:

1. multidisciplinary assessment;
2. an individual development programme;
3. corrective and developmental sessions;
4. consultation with parents or legal representatives and educators.

Support is provided on the basis of a PMPK conclusion.

Under the 2026 rules, organisations providing the state service relating to *rehabilitation and social adaptation of children and adolescents with developmental difficulties* include psychological and pedagogical correction rooms, rehabilitation centres and support centres for children with autism.

This **does not mean** that every child is automatically entitled to a place at any particular organisation. Access depends on the conclusions and eligibility criteria, the way the service is organised locally, and actual availability.

## 5. If your family is considering disability assessment

This is **not** the role of PMPK.

**Medical and social expert assessment** (MSE, `МСЭ`) follows separate rules and is generally based on a referral from a medical organisation's **medical advisory commission** (VKK, `ВКК`).

The MSE rules were amended during 2025 and 2026; the most recent amendments taken into account here took effect on **25 August 2026**.

The rules require three conditions to be present together:

1. a persistent impairment of bodily functions;
2. limitations in everyday functioning;
3. a need for social protection measures.

Therefore, **an autism diagnosis does not automatically establish disability status**.

MSE evaluates functioning and the need for support, not only the name of a diagnosis. For children, a PMPK conclusion may be included in the assessment documents **if available**, but PMPK itself does not determine disability status.

## 6. If you need social services or other support

After the relevant status and eligibility have been established, a family may qualify for measures under Kazakhstan's Social Code.

The Social Code covers, among other matters:

- specialised social services;
- social protection for people with disabilities;
- individual support programmes;
- certain state social benefits;
- selected goods and services supplied through the Social Services Portal.

Eligibility for any particular measure depends on its conditions, an assessment of need and other legal criteria.

Rather than looking for a universal online list of "all autism benefits", ask:

**"What status has been established, which measures are identified for this individual, and which government service applies to our situation?"**

## 7. If you are choosing a private specialist or centre

Private services are a separate consideration. Before paying, establish **exactly what is being offered**:

- healthcare;
- psychological support;
- teaching or educational support;
- social services;
- a learning programme;
- a commercial course not classified as regulated healthcare.

Licensing and qualification requirements differ by service type.

Keep two questions separate:

**Is the organisation legally permitted to provide this service?**

**Is there credible evidence that the particular method is safe and effective for its stated purpose?**

These are not the same question. A licence, diploma or training certificate does not make an unproven intervention evidence-based.

See: [How to check a specialist or autism support centre in Kazakhstan](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/en/help-kazakhstan/verify-specialist-center-kazakhstan/).

## If your child already has an autism diagnosis

You may not need to start every process again. Instead, consider the immediate practical questions:

- Are there any medical concerns requiring follow-up?
- What communication supports are helpful?
- Are adjustments needed in preschool or school?
- Is PMPK assessment appropriate?
- Are there functional limitations that warrant discussing VKK and possible MSE referral with a healthcare provider?
- Which everyday challenges matter most now?
- Which services does the family actually need?

There is also a [Russian-language guide to the first 30 days after an autism diagnosis](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/ru/parents/pervye-30-dney-posle-diagnoza-autizma-v-kazakhstane/).

## Documents worth bringing and keeping

Requirements vary by organisation, but it is useful to keep together:

- identification for the child and legal representative;
- any existing medical reports;
- results of assessments already completed;
- the PMPK conclusion, if available;
- MSE documents and an individual programme, if issued;
- relevant preschool or school documentation;
- a current medication list and significant medical information;
- contact information for professionals already supporting the child.

Do not arrange extensive testing "just in case". PMPK rules specifically restrict the repeated collection of data available to authorities through government digital systems.

## What not to do at the outset

Avoid:

- arranging all possible tests and appointments at once;
- treating a screening result as a medical diagnosis;
- treating a PMPK conclusion as a medical diagnosis;
- assuming disability status follows automatically from autism;
- choosing support by the reputation of a method's name alone;
- paying for a large package of sessions before agreeing on goals;
- taking a photograph of a licence or a course certificate as proof of effectiveness;
- repeating assessments unnecessarily when relevant documentation already exists.

## A simple five-question plan

**1. Is there a health concern or a question about development?**  
Start with healthcare.

**2. Do you need a medical assessment for ASD?**  
Follow the medical diagnostic pathway.

**3. Are special educational conditions or psychological and educational support needed?**  
Consider PMPK and education services.

**4. Are there persistent functional limitations and possible needs for social protection?**  
Discuss VKK and possible MSE referral with your healthcare provider.

**5. Are you planning to pay a private centre?**  
Independently check its legal status, professional qualifications, contract terms and evidence for the proposed approach.

Separating these pathways can prevent unnecessary assessments and help families focus on the support they need.

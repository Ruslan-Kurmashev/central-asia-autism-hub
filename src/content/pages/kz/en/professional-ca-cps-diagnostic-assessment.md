**Jurisdiction:** this is the position of a Canadian professional society. Canadian licensing and referral models do not automatically determine professional authority in Kazakhstan.

## 1. Why a separate diagnostic document is needed

After concerning signs have been identified, the task changes: conduct clinical assessment, consider alternative and co-occurring conditions, determine whether a diagnosis is established and describe necessary support.

CPS addresses excessively long waits for team assessment and proposes a flexible approach in which **the extent of professional involvement depends on case complexity**, rather than a universal model with the same number of practitioners.

## 2. Three models of diagnostic assessment

| CPS model | When considered | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approach 1: lead clinician | Clear clinical presentation and a trained practitioner | Independent diagnosis depends on current local authority and qualifications |
| Approach 2: shared care | More complex or atypical presentation requiring expert consultation | Responsibility is shared between practitioners who exchange information |
| Approach 3: team-based assessment | Complex co-occurring conditions, unclear presentation, need for joint assessment | Team involvement is not always necessary for every child |

The Canadian text allows diagnosis by one specially trained clinician in suitable cases. **This does not authorise any practitioner in Kazakhstan to diagnose ASD independently.**

For children under two, and for less clear or complex presentations, CPS considers shared assessment with another trained practitioner. Diagnosis before age two is possible, but a provisional diagnosis requires timely reassessment. Age alone is not grounds to delay support.

## 3. Main clinical components

The original organises diagnostic work into sequential areas. Read them as **a clinical process structure**, not a mandatory investigation checklist for every child.

| CPS step | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Record review | Previous medical records, assessments and educational observations |
| 2. Interview | Developmental and health history, family context, strengths and goals |
| 3. Assessment of core ASD features | Observation and interaction with the child, diagnostic tools when needed |
| 4. Physical examination | Health assessment and additional investigations where indicated |
| 5. Differential assessment | Other possible explanations and co-occurring conditions |
| 6. Diagnostic decision | Clinical judgement and classification criteria |
| 7. Discussion of findings | Explanation of results and written report |
| 8. Comprehensive assessment for support planning | Functioning, skills, health, needs and child and family priorities |

## 4. Role of diagnostic instruments

CPS mentions standardised clinical instruments including ADOS-2, CARS-2, ADI-R and questionnaires. They differ in age range, duration, training requirements and psychometric characteristics.

**No diagnostic instrument should be interpreted as an independent biomarker or definitive diagnosis.** CPS explicitly states that specialised tool results supplement clinical assessment.

In Kazakhstan, separately check permission to use the instrument, translation quality, validity for the target group and professional authority.

## 5. Differential diagnosis and health

During clinical assessment, CPS recommends considering hearing and vision difficulties, other neurodevelopmental and mental health conditions and medical problems when justified by the assessment findings.

Additional investigations such as EEG, MRI or metabolic testing are considered according to **specific clinical indications**, not as a mandatory package for all children with suspected autism.

An ASD diagnosis does not rule out co-occurring ADHD, anxiety, intellectual characteristics or other medical conditions.

### Later Canadian document: CCMG 2023

Genetic assessment aims to clarify possible causes of developmental characteristics, not to confirm autism from a laboratory result. The Canadian College of Medical Geneticists (CCMG) clarified investigation approaches in a separate 2023 position statement.

| Investigation | CCMG 2023 position |
| --- | --- |
| Chromosomal microarray (CMA) | First tier for ASD, global developmental delay or intellectual disability |
| Fragile X syndrome testing | First tier when characteristic clinical features or family history are present |
| Exome sequencing or comprehensive gene panel | Second tier for global developmental delay or intellectual disability |
| Metabolic investigations | When signs suggest an inherited metabolic disorder |

Exome sequencing should not be presented as mandatory second-tier testing for isolated ASD. A negative genetic result does not exclude autism. Referral, consent, investigation scope and interpretation depend on clinical information, applicable rules and specialist consultation. This updates professional context, not the 2019 CPS document or national prescribing rules for Kazakhstan. [CCMG original](https://doi.org/10.1136/jmg-2022-108962).

## 6. How to communicate findings to families

CPS pays attention to language, accessibility and the quality of discussion. Practitioners should:

- explain the information and observations underlying the conclusion
- distinguish established diagnosis, uncertainty and need for reassessment
- describe strengths, functioning and required support
- discuss family questions, ensure appropriate communication and interpretation if needed
- prepare a clear written report with next steps.

CPS provides for a written report recording the reason for referral, information sources, assessments performed, practitioners involved, diagnostic reasoning, co-occurring conditions and next steps. Explicitly identify a provisional conclusion. Describe needs and recommended referrals separately, along with the follow-up plan. CPS allows telephone or video consultation if an in-person meeting to discuss findings is not possible.

Sharing reports between institutions requires applicable consent and confidentiality safeguards.

## 7. What to do when findings are uncertain

A definitive conclusion is not always possible after the first visit. The original describes options including further information gathering, observation in another setting, expert consultation and reassessment when circumstances change.

**Diagnostic uncertainty does not mean that the child's real needs are absent.** When developmental difficulties exist, consider support and additional indicated assessment without unjustified delay.

### Access to assessment

CPS highlights later identification in girls, language and cultural barriers and shortages of practitioners in remote areas. Collaboration between local clinicians and specialists through telehealth may improve access. This is not permission to diagnose remotely without necessary clinical assessment. Where language barriers exist, arrange interpretation and consider cultural context.

## In brief

Before paying for sessions or a programme, check not only the proposed method but also the **transaction itself**: who receives the money, what is promised, who will work with the child and which documents the family will receive.

This helps reduce the risk of disputes and distinguish a transparent professional service from an unclear commercial offer.

## 1. Find out who you are paying

Ask for the full registered name of the company or the details of the sole proprietor, including the relevant **business identification number (BIN)** or **individual identification number (IIN)** where applicable.

A name used in advertising, on Instagram or on a sign may differ from that of the legal entity that will provide the service and receive payment.

Check that the name on the agreement and payment documents matches the provider you have identified.

## 2. Obtain a clear description of the service before paying

Make sure the written terms explain at least:

- the name and nature of the service;
- who will provide it;
- the length and frequency of sessions;
- the **full price**;
- what is included and excluded;
- cancellation and rescheduling rules;
- conditions for ending the programme;
- whether and how any results or written reports will be provided.

Do not rely only on verbal statements or a promotional page that may later change.

## 3. Check the professional's actual role

Ask about the specialist's **basic professional education and role**, not just their collection of course certificates.

Medical, psychological and educational services are governed by different professional and regulatory requirements. Completion of a short course should not automatically be presented as a complete professional qualification.

## 4. Check licences through the official service where required

For activities that require a licence, use Kazakhstan's **eGov/eLicense** system.

Licence checks establish the legal status of a regulated activity. They **do not establish the scientific effectiveness** of a proposed autism intervention.

See [How to verify a medical licence through eGov and eLicense](/central-asia-autism-hub/kz/en/help-kazakhstan/verify-medical-licence-kazakhstan/) for the practical process.

## 5. Ask for proof of payment

Official consumer protection guidance stresses the importance of retaining documents showing that goods, work or services have been purchased.

Keep, as applicable:

- the **fiscal receipt** or another document confirming payment;
- the service agreement;
- the invoice or payment confirmation;
- written messages describing the terms;
- the advertising offer that influenced your decision.

The format of legally required receipt documents depends on the transaction and applicable rules.

## 6. Separate promises from measurable goals

Be cautious about statements such as:

- "We will make your child speak";
- "We will remove autism";
- "Results guaranteed";
- "You will see an effect after 20 sessions".

Ask for a clear, observable goal instead of a guarantee.

For example: **"The child will be able to independently request a break in three everyday situations."**

A realistic goal should also include an explanation of how progress will be assessed and when the plan will be reviewed.

## 7. Do not buy a large package because of pressure

A "today only" discount, a sense of urgency or the claim that a place will be lost should not replace an informed decision.

For a longer programme, agree beforehand on **when progress will be reviewed** and **when or how the family may end the service**.

## If a dispute arises

1. Keep the agreement, receipts, service description, correspondence, booking records and any evidence of sessions actually provided.
2. Send a **written complaint to the service provider**. Identify the service promised, what happened, the problem and the remedy requested. Retain proof that the complaint was sent and received.
3. Under **Article 42-4 of Kazakhstan's Law on Consumer Protection**, the provider must consider the complaint. If it disagrees with the consumer's demands, it must provide a **reasoned, documented written response within 10 calendar days** of receiving the complaint.
4. If no response is provided within the applicable time limit, or the dispute remains unresolved, consider contacting the territorial consumer protection department, including through the official **e-Otinish (Е-Өтініш)** petition system, or another lawful means of protecting your rights.

See the [official guidance on submitting a complaint (Russian)](https://www.gov.kz/memleket/entities/mti-kzpp/press/region-news/details/27672?lang=ru).

**Important:** the ten-day requirement for responding to a complaint **does not mean every consumer automatically has a right to a full refund**. A refund, price adjustment or compensation depends on the contract, deficiencies, supporting evidence and applicable legal provisions.

## Quick checklist before paying

- [ ] The service provider's registered name and BIN/IIN match the agreement and payment details.
- [ ] The particular service, provider, duration and price are clear.
- [ ] Cancellation, rescheduling, termination and refund terms are understandable.
- [ ] An applicable licence has been verified **if the activity is licensed**.
- [ ] The specialist's education and professional role have been checked.
- [ ] Goals are realistic and the approach to measuring progress is described.
- [ ] It is clear what proof of payment will be issued.
- [ ] The written offer, contract terms and correspondence have been saved.

## Your next step

Before making a payment, undertake **three distinct checks**:

1. **Legal status and documents** relating to the service.
2. **Qualifications** of the individual professional.
3. **Evidence and safety** of the particular method being offered.

Taken together, these checks provide a more complete picture than any one certificate, marketing claim or licence.

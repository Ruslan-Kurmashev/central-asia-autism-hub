## Research findings and essential limitations retained

- Whitehouse et al. 2021, JAMA Pediatrics: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542577/ . 104 randomised infants, 103 analysed, 89 assessed at age three. Of those evaluated at three years, 3/45 met autism criteria in the additional-support group versus 9/44 with usual care. The primary one-sided testing and trial limits are stated, and autism prevention is not claimed.
- Jones et al. 2023, JAMA: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37668621/ . For 475 analysable cases the test sensitivity was 71.0% and specificity 80.7%. Higher values in 335 high-confidence diagnoses are not substituted for the overall result; diagnostic uncertainty in 140 cases is disclosed. Eye tracking is not standalone diagnosis.
- Hviid et al. 2019, Annals: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30831578/ . Nationwide cohort of 657,461 children, 5,025,754 person-years, 6,517 autism diagnoses, adjusted HR 0.93 (95% confidence interval 0.85-1.02). No evidence of increased autism risk from MMR. An HR below one does not prove a protective effect. Cochrane 2021 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8607336/ and WHO December 2025 https://www.who.int/news/item/11-12-2025-statement-gacvs-vaccines-autism are distinguished as other evidence. General vaccine adverse effects are not denied.
- Kirby et al. 2022, Autism Research: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9067163/ . Sensory features were documented for 18,968 (74%) of 25,627 autistic children in US administrative surveillance records. This is not direct measurement of all children or a global prevalence estimate, and associations do not establish causation or intervention efficacy.

Scientific spot-checks and source access limitations were documented in the earlier preparation package. This release is not a new independent full-text expert review or complete retraction surveillance. No evidence-register or human-review status was silently promoted.

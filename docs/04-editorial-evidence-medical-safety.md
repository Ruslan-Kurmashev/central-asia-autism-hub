# Editorial, Evidence, and Medical Safety Policy

**Project:** Central Asia Autism Hub  
**Country implementation:** Autism Hub Kazakhstan  
**Document:** 04 — Editorial, Evidence, and Medical Safety Policy  
**Status:** Approved for the first website version  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Decision date:** 29 July 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document defines a realistic editorial and safety model for the first public version of Autism Hub Kazakhstan.

The project initially operates through one responsible person rather than a multidisciplinary editorial board. The policy therefore does not require unavailable staff, paid reviewers, or a formal peer-review system.

Safety is supported by:

- transparent authorship;
- reliable and traceable sources;
- a repeatable self-review checklist;
- clear limits on high-risk topics;
- proportionate language about evidence and uncertainty;
- visible disclaimers;
- correction and update procedures.

## 2. Editorial responsibility

During the first phase, Ruslan Kurmashev performs the roles of:

- author;
- editor;
- source and reference checker;
- publisher;
- project owner responsible for corrections and updates.

The website must not claim that content has received independent clinical, scientific, legal, or professional review when this has not occurred.

The following statements must not be used without a real documented process:

- “independently reviewed by experts”;
- “clinically reviewed”;
- “approved by a medical board”;
- “official platform recommendations”.

The standard transparency statement is:

> Author and editor: Ruslan Kurmashev. This material is based on the sources listed below. Independent external review was not conducted.

External review may be added later for selected materials. It is not a publication requirement for the initial information resource.

## 3. Editorial workflow

The minimum workflow is:

```text
Topic selection
→ source identification
→ drafting
→ claim-by-claim source check
→ 24–48 hour separation before final review where practical
→ link, wording, conflict, and disclaimer check
→ publication
→ scheduled review or correction
```

The separation between drafting and final review is intended to reduce avoidable errors that are difficult for an author to see immediately after writing.

## 4. Risk-based publication model

### 4.1. Lower-risk content

Examples:

- project descriptions;
- confirmed news and announcements;
- Autism School Kazakhstan programme information;
- bibliographies;
- webinar and event information;
- external course listings;
- general definitions that do not provide medical conclusions.

This content may be published after normal source and wording checks.

### 4.2. Moderate-risk content

Examples:

- child development;
- features that may justify professional consultation;
- screening, assessment, and diagnosis;
- sensory differences;
- sleep, eating, communication, or behaviour;
- general home-support information;
- intervention evidence;
- scientific and research explainers.

This content requires:

- suitable and traceable sources;
- a claim-by-claim review;
- a separate final reading;
- explicit limitations and uncertainty where relevant;
- no individual diagnosis or treatment advice;
- an appropriate informational or medical disclaimer.

Whenever feasible, general medical or developmental guidance should be supported by more than one reliable source. A Research Explained article about a specific study may centre on one publication, but must not convert that study into a clinical recommendation.

### 4.3. High-risk content

Examples:

- medication initiation, discontinuation, or dosage;
- interpretation of an individual child’s symptoms;
- individual treatment or therapy plans;
- urgent neurological, psychiatric, or medical conditions;
- treatment of co-occurring medical conditions;
- individual legal conclusions;
- determining whether autism is present or absent.

Autism Hub Kazakhstan will not provide original individual recommendations in these areas.

High-risk topics may only be addressed through:

- general educational explanations;
- links to suitable official or professional sources;
- clear statements about the limits of website information;
- encouragement to consult an appropriately qualified professional.

When the evidence, source, or appropriate wording is uncertain, publication should be delayed or the claim removed.

## 5. Source priorities

Source selection depends on the question. The general priority is:

1. current official Kazakhstan sources for Kazakhstan-specific law, policy, procedures, and public services;
2. current international or national clinical guidelines;
3. systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
4. well-conducted controlled or observational studies;
5. individual studies;
6. clearly labelled expert commentary;
7. clearly labelled lived experience.

Higher study design alone does not guarantee reliability. Methodological quality, applicability, consistency, precision, funding, and risk of bias also matter.

Social media posts, testimonials, promotional pages, and AI-generated text are not acceptable as evidence for medical or scientific claims.

## 6. Publication checklist

Before publishing substantive parent, medical, scientific, educational, or safety content, the editor checks:

- Is each important factual, medical, or scientific claim supported by a source?
- Is the source suitable for the type of claim?
- Is association clearly separated from causation?
- Is the conclusion wider than the population or setting studied?
- Are data and author interpretation presented separately?
- Are important limitations stated?
- Is statistical significance being confused with practical importance?
- Does any wording sound like an individual diagnosis or prescription?
- Are cure claims, guaranteed outcomes, and fear-based language absent?
- Have DOI values, titles, and external links been checked?
- Is the source corrected, withdrawn, or retracted?
- Are author and publication or update dates present?
- Is the correct disclaimer included?
- Is any relevant conflict of interest disclosed?

The checklist is an internal quality-control tool, not a claim of independent peer review.

## 7. Research Explained

Each Research Explained article follows a stable structure:

1. What did the researchers study?
2. How was the study conducted?
3. Who participated?
4. What did the authors report?
5. What are the main limitations?
6. What might the findings mean?
7. What does the study not demonstrate?
8. Where can the original publication be found?

The article should distinguish:

- observed data from interpretation;
- association from causation;
- statistical significance from practical importance;
- exploratory from confirmatory findings;
- findings in the studied sample from broader generalisation.

Avoid:

- “Scientists found the cause of autism.”
- “A new method has been proven effective.”
- “This analysis can diagnose autism.”
- “The study guarantees improvement.”

Prefer:

- “The study reported an association but does not establish causation.”
- “The findings are preliminary.”
- “The sample was small or narrowly defined.”
- “It is unclear whether the findings apply to other populations.”
- “A single study is not sufficient for a clinical recommendation.”

Preprints may be discussed only when clearly labelled as not peer reviewed. They must not be used as the sole basis for practical medical recommendations.

Original summaries must be written in the project’s own words. Full abstracts, figures, tables, or copyrighted text must not be reproduced without permission or an applicable licence.

## 8. Medical boundaries

The website does not:

- diagnose or exclude autism;
- interpret an individual child’s signs or test results;
- prescribe, recommend dosages, or advise stopping medication;
- provide an individual therapy protocol;
- promise prevention, treatment, recovery, or cure of autism;
- present general home activities as a replacement for assessment or professional support;
- recommend abandoning established medical, developmental, educational, or mental health care.

Home-support materials are limited to general, low-risk educational information with clear limitations.

Screening and assessment tools may be explained, but must not be presented as standalone diagnostic instruments. The MVP does not administer screening questionnaires.

## 9. External courses and learning resources

Autism Hub Kazakhstan does not claim to conduct a full independent audit of every external course.

Before listing an external learning resource, the editor checks publicly available information about:

- organiser identity;
- instructor qualifications where stated;
- intended audience;
- programme description;
- cost;
- evidence and outcome claims;
- cure claims or guaranteed results;
- certification or qualification claims;
- link and registration status;
- relevant financial relationship with the project.

The standard label is:

> External resource. Autism Hub Kazakhstan has not conducted a full independent audit of this programme and does not guarantee its quality. Review the organiser, programme, evidence claims, cost, and participation terms before registering.

Paid content is visibly labelled. Affiliate links and commissions are not used in the MVP.

The initial directory lists educational courses, lectures, webinars, workshops, seminars, and parent or professional education. It does not function as a marketplace for individual child therapy.

## 10. Conflicts of interest

The editor discloses relevant financial and non-financial relationships connected to a topic, method, organisation, course, or service.

Relevant relationships may include:

- employment;
- consulting;
- paid teaching;
- course or service ownership;
- grants or sponsorship;
- partnerships;
- involvement in developing the method being discussed.

A relationship does not automatically prohibit publication, but it must be visible enough for readers to judge potential influence.

Sponsored content must not determine the conclusions of an article. Paid placement or undisclosed promotion is not permitted.

## 11. Translation

The first version supports Russian, Kazakh, and English without a paid translation team.

The practical process may include:

- one approved source-language version;
- AI-assisted or automated translation;
- back-translation to identify major meaning changes;
- manual checking of numbers, names, references, medical terms, warnings, and disclaimers;
- correction when a language problem is reported.

Automated translation alone is not treated as professional language review.

When the editor cannot confidently verify a high-risk translation, publication should be delayed or limited to lower-risk content.

The optional translation notice is:

> This translation was prepared with automated assistance and checked by the project editor for consistency with the source version. Please report possible errors to the project contact address.

## 12. Corrections, updates, and archiving

- Typographical or formatting errors may be corrected without a public notice.
- Changes to facts, figures, conclusions, warnings, or sources are recorded in a short correction or version note.
- Potentially unsafe or substantially incorrect content is temporarily unpublished until corrected.
- Readers are given a contact address for reporting possible errors.
- Core medical, scientific, and parent materials are reviewed approximately once per year.
- Kazakhstan-specific service or process information is checked every 6–12 months where feasible.
- Active external course and event links are checked before listing and periodically while registration is open.
- Research explainers are checked for corrections, expressions of concern, or retractions during scheduled review.

An archived page must be visibly identified as archived and must not appear current.

## 13. Use of artificial intelligence

AI tools may assist with:

- draft structure;
- language editing;
- preliminary translation;
- content classification;
- generation of internal checklists.

AI:

- is not an author;
- is not an evidence source;
- must not invent references;
- does not replace reading original sources;
- does not make final medical or editorial decisions.

The human editor remains responsible for claims, citations, translations, corrections, and publication.

Material AI assistance in content preparation may be disclosed in the editorial policy or on the relevant page when appropriate.

## 14. Information displayed on content pages

Substantive content pages should display:

- author and editor;
- publication date;
- last-updated or last-checked date;
- sources or references;
- relevant conflict-of-interest statement;
- appropriate informational or medical disclaimer;
- correction or version note when a substantive change has occurred.

The site must not display a reviewer field when no independent reviewer participated.

## 15. Future development

External subject-matter, clinical, scientific, legal, language, parent, or autistic reviewers may be added later for selected materials when partnerships, volunteers, or funding become available.

Their participation should be recorded by name, role, scope of review, and date. Future review capacity does not change the transparency requirements for materials published under the initial one-person model.

## 16. Reference framework

This policy is informed by:

- [World Health Organization: Autism](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/autism-spectrum-disorders)
- [NICE: Autism spectrum disorder in under 19s — recognition, referral and diagnosis](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations)
- [NICE: Autism spectrum disorder in under 19s — support and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/CG170)
- [GRADE Working Group](https://www.gradeworkinggroup.org/)
- [ICMJE Recommendations, updated January 2026](https://www.icmje.org/icmje-recommendations.pdf)

These sources guide the editorial framework. They do not create official endorsement of Autism Hub Kazakhstan.

## 17. Final decision

The approved initial model is:

> Autism Hub Kazakhstan operates under a one-person editorial responsibility model. Ruslan Kurmashev serves as author, editor, source checker, and publisher. Independent clinical or scientific review is not claimed. Safety is supported through reliable sources, a standardised self-review checklist, limits on high-risk content, transparent disclaimers, and a visible correction process.

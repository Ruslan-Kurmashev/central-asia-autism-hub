# MVP Scope

**Project:** Central Asia Autism Hub  
**Country implementation:** Autism Hub Kazakhstan  
**Document:** 02 — MVP Scope  
**Status:** Approved for the first website version  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Decision date:** 28 July 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the boundaries of the first public version of Autism Hub Kazakhstan.

The MVP is a parent-first, trilingual information and education resource. It is not an interactive navigation product, clinical service, diagnostic tool, learning management system, or directory marketplace.

The scope prioritises a smaller collection of useful, reviewed, and maintainable resources over a large volume of incomplete or weakly verified content.

## 2. MVP definition

Autism Hub Kazakhstan will provide clear, safe, and evidence-informed information about autism, child development, assessment, everyday support, education, and the responsible evaluation of interventions.

The primary purpose is:

> To help families in Kazakhstan find understandable and trustworthy information about autism and child development, without providing diagnosis, individual medical advice, or treatment promises.

The first version is an information resource. It does not provide personalised pathways or ask users to describe a child’s individual situation.

## 3. Audience priority

The approved audience order is:

1. parents, caregivers, and families in Kazakhstan;
2. educators and professionals;
3. organisations, funders, researchers, and other partners.

The homepage, navigation, language, and content structure must primarily serve parents and families. Professional and organisational content remains available but must not dominate the first user experience.

## 4. Languages

The MVP supports:

- Russian;
- Kazakh;
- English.

Russian and Kazakh have equal priority for parent-facing national content. English is a complete website language for English-speaking families and international audiences.

Core pages must be available in all three languages before public launch. Content must not be presented as translated when a reviewed translation does not exist.

## 5. Parent-facing content areas

The first version will organise parent-facing information into the following subject areas.

### 5.1. Understanding autism

- what autism is;
- neurodevelopment and neurodiversity;
- respectful terminology;
- common myths and facts;
- individual variation among autistic people.

### 5.2. Child development and early questions

- speech and language development;
- social communication;
- play and interaction;
- behaviour and regulation;
- sensory differences;
- situations in which professional consultation may be appropriate.

Content in this area must not function as a diagnostic checklist or calculate the likelihood of autism.

### 5.3. Assessment and diagnosis

- the difference between screening, assessment, and diagnosis;
- roles of relevant professionals and services;
- preparation for a consultation;
- developmental and medical history;
- the role of PMPK in the educational pathway;
- limits of online information and screening tools.

### 5.4. After an assessment or diagnosis

- realistic first steps for a family;
- understanding recommendations;
- setting functional and measurable support goals;
- organising records and questions;
- communicating with professionals;
- avoiding urgency-driven or fear-based decisions.

### 5.5. Everyday support

- communication in daily routines;
- predictable routines and transitions;
- behaviour as communication;
- emotional and sensory regulation;
- sleep and eating concerns;
- safe, low-risk activities at home.

General home-support content must not be presented as individual therapy or a substitute for professional assessment.

### 5.6. Kindergarten, school, and inclusion

- communication with educators;
- preparation for PMPK and educational discussions;
- inclusive education principles;
- reasonable adaptations and participation;
- transitions between educational settings;
- respectful handling of bullying and stigma.

Country-specific legal or procedural claims require current source verification before publication.

### 5.7. Interventions, evidence, and safety

- how to evaluate intervention claims;
- questions to ask service providers;
- realistic outcomes and monitoring;
- conflicts of interest;
- unsupported, potentially harmful, or misleading claims;
- the distinction between support, treatment of co-occurring conditions, and promises to “cure autism”.

### 5.8. Parent and family wellbeing

- stress and uncertainty;
- family communication;
- burnout and support needs;
- finding peer and professional support;
- avoiding blame and stigma.

This content is informational and must not replace individual mental health care or crisis support.

### 5.9. Finding help in Kazakhstan

The MVP may explain the general roles of healthcare, assessment, PMPK, education, non-governmental organisations, and family-support resources in Kazakhstan.

It may link to a limited number of current, authoritative, and verified resources. It will not initially operate a comprehensive city directory, professional database, service ranking, or interactive map.

### 5.10. Autism School Kazakhstan

The MVP will present:

- the purpose of Autism School Kazakhstan;
- its parent and family audience;
- educational principles;
- planned or available topic areas;
- reviewed educational materials that are ready for public use;
- information about lectures, webinars, or future learning opportunities when confirmed.

The first version will not operate as an LMS and will not track course completion.

## 6. Initial content volume

The target for launch is **8–12 core parent-facing resources**, each available in Russian, Kazakh, and English.

Quality requirements take priority over volume. A resource is ready for publication only when:

- its purpose and audience are clear;
- medical, developmental, educational, or legal claims are supported by suitable sources;
- the required risk-based editorial review defined in Document 04 has been completed;
- all three language versions have been reviewed;
- the author and editor, any external reviewer when applicable, publication date, and last-updated date are recorded;
- references and disclaimers are present;
- the content provides information without individual diagnosis or treatment advice.

The website will be described as a regularly expanding library of reviewed resources. It will not claim to contain all available autism information.

## 7. Content for professionals

Professional content is a secondary part of the MVP.

It may include:

- evidence-informed reference materials;
- guidance on respectful communication with families;
- ethical and safety principles;
- selected resources and external guidelines;
- information about future lectures or professional education.

Professional content must be clearly separated from parent-facing guidance and must not imply certification, accreditation, or regulatory endorsement.

## 8. MVP capabilities

The first website version may provide:

- browsing by topic;
- language switching;
- structured articles and educational resources;
- related-content links;
- accessible navigation and page layouts;
- downloadable resources only when an accessible, reviewed version is available;
- links to verified external resources;
- a direct project email address.

The MVP does not require site search, user accounts, forms, personalisation, or persistent user state.

## 9. Explicitly excluded from the MVP

The following are outside the approved first-version scope:

- an interactive Parent Navigator;
- personalised pathways or recommendations;
- questions about an individual child;
- developmental or autism screening questionnaires;
- diagnostic probability calculations;
- collection or storage of child or family information;
- medical records or clinical histories;
- user registration and accounts;
- an LMS or course-progress tracking;
- quizzes, certificates, or professional accreditation;
- paid courses or subscriptions;
- individual consultations;
- comments, forums, or parent chat;
- a comprehensive specialist or organisation database;
- ratings, rankings, or paid placement;
- an interactive service map;
- claims of diagnosis, treatment, prevention, cure, or guaranteed outcomes;
- dedicated country platforms outside Kazakhstan;
- native mobile applications;
- first-party video hosting.

## 10. City and service information

Comprehensive city pages are deferred until a repeatable verification and maintenance process exists.

A future city page will require:

- verified contact information;
- a visible verification date;
- an assigned person or organisation responsible for updates;
- transparent inclusion criteria;
- a process for reporting incorrect or outdated information;
- clear separation between factual listing and endorsement.

The initial MVP will provide general Kazakhstan-focused system information and verified links rather than incomplete city catalogues.

## 11. MVP readiness criteria

The first version is ready for public launch when:

1. the approved core pages are available in Russian, Kazakh, and English;
2. 8–12 core parent-facing resources satisfy the publication requirements;
3. the required editorial and language checks defined in Documents 04 and 06 are documented, together with any external review when applicable;
4. the website does not collect child, health, or family data;
5. no content provides individual diagnosis or treatment recommendations;
6. authors and editors, any external reviewers when applicable, sources, publication dates, update dates, and disclaimers are visible where required;
7. external links and Kazakhstan-specific information have been checked;
8. the website is usable on mobile devices and with keyboard navigation;
9. critical pages meet the approved accessibility requirements;
10. the production build succeeds for the approved hosting environment.

## 12. Deferred development

The following may be considered after the information resource, editorial process, and content-maintenance model are operating reliably:

- a non-diagnostic Parent Navigator;
- verified city resource pages;
- a maintained organisation directory;
- webinars and structured learning cohorts;
- an LMS;
- privacy-respecting analytics;
- feedback forms;
- additional Central Asian country implementations.

Each deferred capability requires a separate scope, safety, privacy, maintenance, and validation decision before implementation.

## 13. Final decision

The approved MVP model is:

> Autism Hub Kazakhstan will launch as a parent-first, trilingual information and education resource. It will provide reviewed and evidence-informed materials for families, with secondary content for professionals. The first version will not include personalised navigation, diagnostic functions, an LMS, user accounts, or collection of child and family data.

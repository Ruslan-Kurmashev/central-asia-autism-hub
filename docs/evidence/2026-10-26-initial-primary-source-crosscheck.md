# Initial source-level review: selected ASD research claims

Date: 2026-10-26
Scope: four explicitly quoted claims in two published Research Explained articles.
Outcome: primary-source numerical comparisons performed, editor sign-off still pending.
Registry: `docs/evidence/claims-register.csv`

## Hviid et al. (2019), doi:10.7326/M18-2101

Primary research article: https://doi.org/10.7326/M18-2101
PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30831578/

- The study describes a nationwide Danish cohort of 657461 children born 1999-2010 and 6517 autism diagnoses during follow-up.
- Reported adjusted hazard ratio comparing MMR vaccinated with unvaccinated: 0.93 (95% confidence interval 0.85 to 1.02).
- The interval includes 1 and does not establish a protective effect.
- Limitations: observational register data, no individual medical charts reviewed. The source itself does not establish individual causal effects.
- Registry claims ASD-0001 and ASD-0002 match the article's existing `keyPoints`.

## Whitehouse et al. (2021), doi:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2021.3298

Original open-access paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8453361/
PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542577/

- The Australian randomized trial recruited 104 infants aged 9-14 months, excluding one participant after randomization for its analyzed sample of 103.
- At age 3, reassessment was available for 89 participants: 3 of 45 in iBASIS-VIPP and 9 of 44 with usual care met autism diagnostic criteria.
- Main outcome analyses were prespecified with one-sided tests, with interpretation limitations noted by the authors.
- Results are about one intervention and measured outcomes, not prevention or cure of autism.
- Registry claims ASD-0003 and ASD-0004 match the article's existing `keyPoints`.
